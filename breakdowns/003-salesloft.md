# Salesloft Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Salesloft blog post "9 Constructive B2B Cold Email Templates" (published 2022, updated Nov 2024)
**Template number:** 003 (one source, 9 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** not provided per template. The post gives general subject line guidance only.
**Performance data:** Salesloft reports three results for its own inbound SDR team: a 16% reply rate for the website visit template, a 44% open rate for the voicemail follow-up, and a 17.9% reply rate for the demo follow-up. It also says the case study template is its most popular, with no number. Other claims: one-word subject lines get an 87% higher reply rate, and personalizing even 20% of emails can double response rates. All of these are Salesloft's own figures and were not independently verified. Inbound SDR results apply to warm leads, so they do not predict cold outbound results.

> Note on the source: the 9 templates are generic fill-in-the-blank patterns. Only some are true cold emails. Templates 2, 7 and 8 are warm follow-ups (a website visitor, a voicemail, a demo). The page has no emails in images. Everything below is paraphrase.

## The 9 templates in this source

| # | Template | Core idea | Word count (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Useful resource | Shares a fresh market resource with an attachment, asks for nothing, offers more help | 45 |
| 2 | Website visit | Acknowledges their interest, includes a short intro video and a teaser | 45 |
| 3 | Case study | Leads with a named customer's results and a link, asks for a call on one of two specific days | 55 |
| 4 | Content promotion | Says many people in their role share a problem, links an article, asks what they think | 55 |
| 5 | Collaboration | Compliments a podcast with a mutual contact, offers alternative ideas, asks for a 10 minute call | 85 |
| 6 | AIDA | Mentions their LinkedIn post, names a shared roadblock, links research, invites them to call | 95 |
| 7 | Voicemail follow-up | References the voicemail and offers two time options | 30 |
| 8 | Demo follow-up | Thanks them, summarizes the product, lists pricing and resources | 170 |
| 9 | Pain point | Asks about their current process, says other organizations are investing in the technology, asks if they would be against hearing how peers use it | 75 |

## Who it targets and why it fits

Business buyers in general, written from the view of a sales engagement vendor. Templates 2, 7 and 8 fit prospects who already engaged. Templates 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 9 fit true cold outreach to managers and executives who read a lot of vendor email.

## Structure

- **Resource first (1, 4, 6):** give something useful, then a light line.
- **Proof first (3):** open with a customer result and a link.
- **Personal connection (5, 6):** open with something they said or published.
- **Warm follow-up (2, 7, 8):** refer to the earlier touch and move the conversation forward.
- **Question first (9):** open with a question about their current process, then peer behavior, then a soft ask.
- The post also names the classic copywriting frame AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action) as one template.

## Psychology used, and why it works here

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 (resource, video, case study, research) | Gives value first, so a reply feels fair |
| Specificity | All nine in some form (market trend, visit, customer result, podcast, LinkedIn post, voicemail) | Shows the message is about them |
| Social proof | 3 (named customer and link), 8 (many companies), 9 (other organizations are investing) | Borrowed credibility and a bandwagon effect |
| Normalization (shared problem) | 4, 6 ("many people in your role", "we both know") | Makes the problem feel common, so admitting it is easy |
| Forced choice (two named time slots) | 3, 7 | Picking between two options is easier than answering an open meeting request |
| Liking through a specific compliment | 5 (podcast with a mutual contact) | Real flattery builds rapport, and the mutual contact adds borrowed trust |
| Authority | 1 (data team), 6 (own research) | Original data signals expertise |
| Personal video (humanization) | 2 | Puts a face on the sender and makes the email feel personal |
| No-oriented question | 9 (asks if they would be against hearing more) | A no is easy to give, which lowers pressure and can invite a reply |
| Consistency across channels | 7 | Refers to a previous touch, so the email feels like part of one conversation |
| Transparency | 8 (open pricing) | Removes a common unknown, but is too much for a first email |
| Commitment minimization | 1, 2, 4, 5, 9 | The ask is small or missing |
| Curiosity gap | 2 (teaser), 5 (ideas), 6 (research), 9 (how peers use it) | Hints at something without giving it all |

## Hook type and subject line pattern

- **Hooks:** fresh resource, visitor acknowledgment, customer result, shared problem, podcast compliment, LinkedIn post, voicemail reference.
- **Subject lines:** none given. Salesloft's guidance, with its own unverified numbers, is to use one-word subjects, leave out urgency, punctuation and emojis, and personalize.

## CTA type

A wide mix: no ask or an open offer of help (1, 8), no ask beyond a video (2), a two-day meeting ask (3, 7), an opinion ask (4), a 10 minute call (5), an interest question plus a phone number (6), a no-oriented question (9). Several templates also include attachments or links, which add friction.

## Personalization method

Behavioral and content-based: website visits, downloads, podcasts, LinkedIn posts, voicemails and demos. The post recommends automating generic emails and spending the saved time personalizing the most important ones.

## Length, reading level, tone

Most run 30 to 95 words. Template 8 is much longer (about 170). Plain words, friendly, casual, with a few emojis. The post tells writers to avoid corporate and spammy language.

## Weaknesses and what could be better

- 1 attaches a file to a first email and asks for nothing. A short link or inline insight plus a clear small question would work better.
- 2 depends on warm intent. Without a visit, "you might be interested" reads as presumptuous. It also has no explicit ask.
- 3 uses hype wording and asks for a meeting in a first email. The forced-choice days help, but a smaller first ask would be safer.
- 4 opens with a generic "many people in your role" line. Naming one real, specific problem would be stronger.
- 5 needs a real mutual contact and a real podcast. "Alternative strategies" is vague and implies their current approach is wrong.
- 6 stacks three asks (a link, their thoughts, a phone call), and "we both know" is presumptuous. One CTA is better.
- 7 is fine for a warm follow-up but gives no reason to meet.
- 8 is a feature and pricing dump with attachments. Good for a warm demo recap, bad for a cold email.
- 9 relies entirely on the personalization placeholder. A question phrased around being "against" something can confuse the reader.
- Stats like the 87% subject line lift have no method shown.
