# SmartWriter Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** SmartWriter blog post "16 Cold Email Templates You Should Know If You're In Sales" (May 2021), from an AI email writing vendor
**Template number:** 009 (one source, 21 emails analyzed: 16 text templates plus 5 emails shown as screenshots; 2 more screenshots are weak AI demo outputs and were not counted)
**Subject lines:** provided for some templates (quoted below)
**Performance data:** mostly not provided. The one template-level figure: the "10 minutes" direct pitch is said to have drawn a 57% open rate and a 21% response rate, with no source or sample size. Guide-level claims, all unverified: cold email accounts for 5 to 7% of sales, reps who use cold email generate up to 53% more leads (attributed to Salesforce), and subject lines of four words or fewer performed best (attributed to Leadium). SmartWriter also claims 80% open and 40% reply rates for its own tool's output, which is a product claim.

> Note on the source: this is a 2021 vendor blog, and several templates are weak by modern standards. Four of them name well-known entrepreneurs as customers, which only works if true and permitted. Some of the "screenshot" emails are not cold emails at all (a product beta invite and a newsletter). The page has 14 images: five are real email screenshots, two are AI demo outputs, and the rest are banners, stock art and product screens. Everything below is paraphrase.

## Who it targets and why it fits

Sellers of software, services and collaborations: sales reps, link builders, creators and marketers. Many templates assume a busy executive and offer to give back time. The post's own conclusion is that templates help, but personalization raises response rates more.

## Structure

Most templates follow named copywriting frames: AIDA, Before-After-Bridge, Problem-Agitate-Solve and Praise-Picture-Push. Others are practical situation templates (event, visitor, wrong person, introduction, influencer, video review). The post's three-point checklist for a good email: address the prospect directly, be specific and relevant, and mention a common contact.

## Hook type and subject line pattern

- **Hooks:** a recent event, a competitor in use, a profile or post, a talk, a website visit, an article, a channel or video, a common contact.
- **Subject patterns:** a one-word congratulation, a topic label ("Time Saving Software"), a number-based promise, a resource line, a name-plus-company collaboration, a reference to their content, an exclusive invite.
- **Guidance (unverified):** keep subjects to four words or fewer, avoid common spam phrases, and sound like a person.

## CTA type

A 10 minute call or demo (about half), a call with a specific time and a calendar link, two time options, an interest question, a request to introduce you, a free account for an honest review, a yes-or-no on a ready draft, and a click on a button (the beta invite).

## Personalization method

Manual research (events, profiles, posts, videos, websites). The vendor also pushes its AI tool, which scrapes pages for personalization, but its own demo outputs are generic and show that AI without real research produces weak "big fan" emails.

## Follow-up sequence

Not covered in detail. The post says to send follow-ups when there is no reply.

## Length, reading level, tone

Most are 50 to 95 words, with a few longer (the right-person and video review templates run 130 to 140). Plain words, friendly and slightly informal, with some awkward phrasing and a few emoticons.

## Weaknesses and what could be better

- **Fake-looking name-drops.** Four templates cite famous entrepreneurs as customers. Unless real and permitted, that is a fake claim and should never be copied.
- **Repetitive pitch.** Several templates reuse the same "save 10 hours a week" claim with a demo ask, so they read alike.
- **Presumptuous claims.** The competitor template says the product is the same as the one they use but better, and the BAB example assumes the prospect is losing customers. Both assume facts that may be wrong.
- **Negative framing.** The BAB example opens with a flaw and an exclamation mark in the subject line. That can feel like an accusation or spam.
- **Mismatched examples.** The Vimeo and newsletter screenshots are not cold emails to strangers, so they teach less about cold outreach than they appear to.
- **Contradiction.** The video review template offers a free account with no obligation and then asks for a review in return.
- **Generic flattery.** "Big fan of your product" with no specific detail (shown in the AI demo outputs) is a classic weak opener.
- **Unsourced claims.** The 57% open rate, the 53% lead lift and the 5 to 7% share have no stated source or method.
- **Dated.** One template refers to a 2014 conference, and the post is from 2021.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The good-versus-bad annotated pair: personalization, a common contact and a specific ask versus a generic hello and a generic ask.
- The ready-draft guest post pitch: finished work up front plus a simple yes-or-no question.
- The introduction request and the audience swap, which give the other person a reason to say yes.
- The right-person email that shows real research before asking for a redirect.
- Praise-Picture-Push as a way to pair a real compliment with a relevant offer.
