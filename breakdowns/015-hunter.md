# Hunter Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Hunter.io email template directory (43 listing pages, 511 templates listed, 510 captured because one link was a duplicate)
**Template number:** 015 (one source, 510 templates)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (a few are quoted below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The directory gives no reply rates or results.

> How I analyzed it: I captured all 510 templates (subject, body and category tags), ran keyword and length analysis across all of them, and read in full a stratified sample of 124 (two from every subcategory, and extras from the four largest). Counts for the whole library are therefore keyword-based estimates. The page has no emails in images. Everything below is paraphrase.

## Who it targets and why it fits

A very wide audience: sellers, recruiters, marketers, link builders, job seekers and customer success teams. A large part of the library is not cold outreach at all (invoices, interview logistics, offboarding, rejections, renewals), so only the sales, networking, SEO, marketing and recruitment-outreach groups matter for this skill.

## Structure

Typical cold templates are short and plain: a custom opener tied to a specific detail, one line on why you are writing, one proof or idea, and a small ask. The curated set follows this consistently, with a custom field in the subject and several fields in the body (about five placeholders per template). The community set is looser and often leads with the sender.

## Hook type and subject line pattern

- **Hooks (from the sample):** a specific post, talk or article, a visible product or hiring change, a pricing page visit, a webinar they missed, a congratulation, a mutual contact, a team-page or tech-stack signal, and a prior email.
- **Subject patterns:** a custom detail in the subject (the curated style), "following up: [topic]", a short congratulation, a plain quick question, and the person's own content ("your [talk] on [topic]"). The older community set uses more generic or gimmicky subjects.

## CTA type

About 40% mention a call, chat or coffee, about 9% name a time like 15 minutes, 7% mention a calendar link, and about 36 offer a lighter alternative (answer by email, one specific question, a one-line reply). Only a small share offer two specific time options.

## Personalization method

The curated set uses fill-in fields throughout (a specific insight, a recent trigger, a particular project) and insists on specificity: it tells the sender to be concrete and not vague. Fields are the main personalization mechanism, with no automation described.

## Follow-up sequence

The library has follow-up templates for no response, meetings, events, webinars, proposals and job applications. Several follow-ups offer a different angle or a lighter alternative instead of repeating the ask, and some are break-ups that say you will stop.

## Length, reading level, tone

The curated set is short and polished, around grade 7 to 9 and respectful. The community set has more typos, odd phrasing, long product lists and some outdated references.

## Weaknesses and what could be better

- **A very wide scope dilutes it.** Many templates (invoices, offboarding, interview logistics, renewals) are not cold email, so counts of "emails" overstate cold outreach.
- **Mixed quality.** The community set has stock openers, typos, product-heavy paragraphs, long emails, and in some cases manipulative or off-putting examples: a request for a postal address from a stranger, a joke about the sender's own tracking software, a sports metaphor pitch, and a pandemic-era template.
- **Fields can hide vagueness.** A template with five fields is only as good as the details you fill in. Weak details make it generic.
- **No results.** There is no evidence of which templates perform.
- **Heavy reliance on calls.** About two fifths ask for a call, while the lighter-ask templates show a better way.
- **A "free" in some community templates.** About 16% use the word in the body, which can trigger spam filters.
- **Overlap.** Six variants per subcategory share a structure, so the library is less varied than its count suggests.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The curated set's discipline: short, specific, a custom detail in the subject and no stock opener.
- The lighter ask: an email-only answer, one specific question, or a one-sentence reply.
- The internship pitch with a concrete project idea, and the speculative job inquiry.
- The congratulation with no ask, and reconnecting through their content.
- The webinar follow-up that offers the best five-minute clip instead of the whole recording.
- The lighter-alternative follow-up that changes the offer instead of repeating the ask.
