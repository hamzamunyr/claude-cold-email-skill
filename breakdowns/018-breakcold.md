# Breakcold Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Breakcold blog "15 Cold Email Templates Analyzed [With Results]" (updated Sep 2026), by a CRM vendor's CEO
**Template number:** 018 (one source, 16 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for 15 of the 16 (quoted below)
**Performance data:** not provided. Despite "with results" in the title, the page gives the author's opinions about each template (open rate, reply rate, deliverability, call to action), not measured results. The claims are anecdotal: a personalized first line is said to lift open rate to over 60%, an "interview mode" email to lift replies by 15 to 20%, and a community invite to have added over 100 group members in 5 days. None is verified.

> Note on the source: the title says 15, the page has 16 templates, and the images are numbered up to 30. I checked the images: they are screenshots of the same templates, with a few extra one-line author notes, so they add no new emails. Many examples use joke names and companies. Everything below is paraphrase.

## Who it targets and why it fits

Founders, small agencies and sellers of tools who send high volume outreach and want short, casual emails. Most templates are for SEO, link building, community building and light sales. The author's advice is to keep emails under about 75 words with one ask.

## Structure

Short and casual: a custom first line (the page calls it an icebreaker), one idea, one question. The author scores each template on open rate, reply rate, sales pitch, subject line, deliverability and call to action, and flags images and links as deliverability risks.

## Hook type and subject line pattern

- **Hooks:** noticing an absence ("you are not using X"), a post or interest they showed, a mention online, a named employee, a city, a broken link, an authority range, a research pretext, a referrer.
- **Subject patterns:** lowercase and casual subjects chosen to stand out in an inbox, a plain "quick question", a reading-time gimmick, "should we explore?", and short topical lines. The author says lowercase subjects break the inbox pattern.

## CTA type

A short question or "worth a chat?" (most), an offer to send a case study, a call this week or next, a link to join, a yes-or-no question, and a coffee or call offer.

## Personalization method

A variable first line (icebreaker), plus variables for a page slug, an employee name, a city or an interest post. The author notes the employee variable takes time but is worth it. Tools in the page help generate lines.

## Follow-up sequence

Not covered. The page is only first-touch templates.

## Length, reading level, tone

About 30 to 95 words, very casual, reading level around grade 6, with jokes and informal greetings.

## Weaknesses and what could be better

- **The title overpromises.** "[With Results]" and "15" are both off. There are no measured results, and the count is 16.
- **Pretexts.** The research interview email says the sender is writing an article. If that is not true, it is deceptive.
- **Jokes that can misfire.** "A "just joking" line and bar humor can confuse or irritate a prospect. The author himself calls some first lines aggressive.
- **Images and links.** Several templates use memes, signature images or many links, which the author admits can hurt deliverability.
- **Surveillance feel.** Referring to a prospect's tweet, their employee or their mention online can feel invasive unless handled lightly.
- **Pushy call ask.** Template 6 stacks named clients, examples, links and a call ask, which the author calls sales-heavy.
- **Anecdotal claims.** The percentage lifts and the community growth have no method.
- **Joke examples.** The examples use fictional names and companies, which can hide how a real email would read.
- **Vendor framing.** The page promotes the author's CRM.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The curiosity-gap question that does not sell.
- The interest-signal case study offer with the post shown in the P.S.
- The broken-link and authority-swap emails for link building.
- The casual one-liner for early products.
- The notes format: for each template, a quick read on open rate, reply rate, deliverability and ask.
- The advice to keep emails short and to treat images and links as a trade-off.
