# Truelist Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Truelist blog "6 Powerful Cold Email Templates for Sales That Get Replies", from an email verification vendor
**Template number:** 019 (one source, 9 templates analyzed. The title says 6, the page has 9.)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (quoted below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page gives unverified benchmarks: 3 to 8% reply rate is normal, 8 to 15% is good, over 15% is exceptional, and under 2% usually points to deliverability or list quality. It also claims a Primary-inbox email with 52% opens beats a Promotions one at 18%.

> Note on the source: this is a vendor page for an email verification service, so it stresses deliverability. The four images have alt text that only repeats template names, and the image host timed out, so I could not view them. All nine email bodies are in the page text. Everything below is paraphrase.

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers and SDR teams. The page frames six foundational frameworks (value proposition, social proof, question, PAS, referral, content first) plus three modern ones for AI personalization, multi-channel and account-based outreach.

## Structure

Each template has the body copy, a strategic breakdown and actionable takeaways. The page adds three things that matter before any template: authentication and sender requirements, the fact that AI-generated personalization is easy to spot, and the fact that deliverability beats clever copy.

## Psychology used, and why it works here

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction | All 9 are under about 110 words | Quick to read |
| Commitment minimization | All 9 (a 15 minute call, a question, a document) | Small asks |
| Specificity | About 6 (a post, a podcast, a comment, a funding round) | Shows it was written for them |
| Social proof and quantified benefit | 7 and 4 (named clients, metrics) | Borrowed credibility and concrete gains |
| Reciprocity and lighter ask | 4 each (a guide, a write-up, a one-pager, "no call needed") | Gives value and lowers effort |
| Normalization | 3 ("companies in your space often struggle") | Makes the problem feel common |
| Borrowed trust | 2 (the mutual connection templates) | A shared contact adds credibility |
| Self-aware honesty | 2 (admitting it may not fit their direction, sharing lessons learned) | Admitting limits builds trust |
| No-ask framing | 1 (the content-first email says there is no pitch today) | Removes pressure |
| Problem agitation | 1 (PAS) | Makes the cost vivid |
| Intent signal and consistency | 1 each (a funding window, a prior LinkedIn comment) | A reason to write now, and a continuation |

## Hook type and subject line pattern

- **Hooks:** a mutual connection, a LinkedIn post, a podcast or panel appearance, a prior LinkedIn comment, a funding round, a shared problem.
- **Subject patterns:** a quick question, an idea with a connection's name, a better way, a few thoughts, "your point on...", a follow-up on a comment, and a funding-plus-bottleneck line.

## CTA type

A 15 minute call (three templates, one with two day options), a brief chat, an offer to send a document or playbook (four templates, one with "no call needed"), and no ask at all (the content-first email).

## Personalization method

Research-driven. The page says AI should find signals at scale (podcasts, hiring spikes, launches) and a human writes the sentence. It advises quoting the specific thing and skipping compliments.

## Follow-up sequence

Three to five touches spaced three to five business days apart, then stop. The page warns that long sequences with several "bumping this up" emails get spam-flagged.

## Length, reading level, tone

About 75 to 105 words, plain and professional, around grade 8.

## Weaknesses and what could be better

- **The title says 6, the page has 9.**
- **Placeholder numbers.** Metrics inside templates (a 35% lift, a 40% cut) are illustrative, and the real figures must be true.
- **Hard to scale.** The stage-targeted and AI-assisted templates need per-account research, which the page admits.
- **Several templates stay generic.** The value proposition and question templates rely on filler like "I work with" and "companies like yours".
- **FOMO framing.** Using a competitor's success to create urgency can feel manipulative if the link is weak.
- **Vendor framing.** The page pushes verified addresses, which is relevant but self-serving.
- **No results.** Nothing shows which template performs.

## What is genuinely useful here

- "Use AI to draft, never to ship", and the advice to quote a specific thing and skip compliments.
- The multi-channel soft pivot, built on a real earlier touch.
- The stage hypothesis, and volunteering what you would do differently.
- Offering the asset, not the meeting.
- Getting permission before naming a client or a mutual connection.
- The cadence guidance and the point that deliverability does more work than copy.
