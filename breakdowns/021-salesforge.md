# Salesforge Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Salesforge blog "10 NEW Cold Email Templates That (Actually!) Get Replies", from an AI cold email platform
**Template number:** 021 (one source, 10 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (quoted below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page cites two customer results (an 85% positive reply rate and 16%+ across 214K prospects) that I could not verify, and neither is tied to a template.

> Note on the source: a vendor page. The images are avatars and a product screenshot, so all email bodies are in the text. Everything below is paraphrase except the subject lines.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Who it targets and why it fits

Sellers, agencies, creators, students, job seekers and partnership builders. Only templates 1, 2, 5 and 6 are sales; the other six are non-sales uses of the same cold structure.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction | All 10 | Short, one idea per line |
| Commitment minimization | 9 (a quick chat, a 15 or 20 minute call, coffee) | Small asks |
| Specificity | 8 (a recent event, their content, a channel) | Shows research |
| Social proof and quantified benefit | 5 and 4 (a similar company's result) | Borrowed credibility |
| Curiosity gap | 3 ("spotted something", "blind spot") | Makes them want to know |
| Liking through compliment | 3 (their content, product, work) | Rapport |
| Normalization | 2 ("most companies hit this wall") | Makes the problem common |
| Mutual benefit | 2 (affiliate, partnership) | Win-win framing |
| Similarity | 2 (networking, mentorship) | Shared path |
| Reciprocity | 2 (insights first) | Gives before asking |
| No-ask framing and convenience | 1 each ("no pitch", coming to them) | Lowers pressure and effort |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a recent growth event or win (4), their content (1), their digital presence (1), a career path (1).
- **CTAs:** a short call or chat (6), coffee (2), a direct question about a program (1), an offer to help on any task (1).
- **Personalization:** one researched detail, then a similar-company result. The page stresses research before writing, referencing a post or trigger event, and pre-headers that add context.

## Sequence and cadence

Not given per template. The page's 30/30/50 rule says to spend 30% of time on research, 30% on personalization and 50% on follow-up, since most replies come from follow-ups.

## Weaknesses

- **Placeholder results.** Templates rely on "a similar company saw X", which must be true.
- **Vague hook.** "I noticed something most companies miss" never says what, which can read as bait.
- **A typo** in template 9's subject in the source.
- **Self-contradiction.** The page says to cut stock openers, yet several templates begin with a "love what you've built" line.
- **Vendor framing and unverified claims.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- One non-sales shape per goal (networking, mentorship, partnership).
- "No pitch, just insights" paired with a named duration.
- Subjects that add specificity, and pre-headers that add context instead of repeating.
- The research / personalization / follow-up time split.
