# Salesbread Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Salesbread blog "24 Best Personalized Cold Email Templates (With Tips)", from a lead generation agency
**Template number:** 023 (one source, 31 emails analyzed: 24 templates, 3 short examples of compliment, commonality and question, and 4 screenshot emails)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page says personalized emails get more replies, based on its own analysis of 75 million emails, but gives no template-level numbers, so I treat it as unverified.

> Note on the source: an agency page. I viewed all the screenshots (a bad bump email, two podcast outreach emails, a short beta-community email, a follow-up that admits a forgotten link, and an A/B test diagram). Template bodies are very short. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The 24 templates by group

| Group | Subjects | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Collaboration (5) | "Re: Partnership Opportunities", "Partnership Opportunities", "Marketing Collaboration with {Your Company Name}?", "Guest Contribution", "Let's Chat About a Potential Collaboration" | A numbered reply menu, a peer-proof partnership ask with a one-pager, a teaching invite, a free article offer, a vague quick call |
| Follow-up (5) | "Call", "Voicemail", "Next Step?", "Reconnecting", "Goodbye?" | A reminder of an earlier request, a voicemail note, a still-relevant question, a reconnect question, a closing-files line |
| Playful (2) | "Yes/No?", "Quick Question, {First Name}!" | Three possible reasons for silence, a humorous reply-A-or-B choice |
| Authority (3) | "Google's Only Recommended CRM", "{First Name}, quick idea for {Company}", "{First Name}, we did this for Uber & Shopify" | Big-brand claims, a result placeholder, an offer of a breakdown or a chat |
| Warm outreach (3) | "Loved Your Work - Would Love to Connect!", "Would Love to Have You as a Speaker!", "Would Love to Feature You in Our Podcast" | A generic profile compliment, a speaker invite, an interview invite |
| Product and demo (4) | "Salesbread App", "Feedback Tool for {company}", "Interested in Learning More?", "A Personal Thank You" | A reply-yes offer, a free trial, a post-demo chat, a thank-you with a feedback ask |
| SEO and content (2) | "SEO?", "Re: {Last Subject-line}" | A link to a thread and a question, a check on a sent resource with a 7 minute ask |

Most are 25 to 70 words. The three short CCQ examples and the screenshots follow the same one-idea shape.

## Who it targets and why it fits

Mostly B2B sellers and outreach agencies. The groups cover partnership, follow-up, social proof, networking, product and content outreach, so the page works as a pick-your-situation library.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a prior request or touch (6), their content (3), a website visit, a mutual connection, a thank-you after a demo or signup, a directory listing.
- **CTAs:** a short call of 5 to 15 minutes (8), a reply with a letter or "yes" (5), an interest question (5), an interview or feature request (2), a free trial (1).
- **Personalization:** the page teaches CCQ (compliment, commonality, question) and a custom intro of one or two sentences, plus a P.S. that adds value, not a pitch.

## Sequence and cadence

Send one email and one follow-up per micro-campaign and test two lists. Space follow-ups using the Fibonacci sequence. Do not use a bare "did you see my email" bump: make it add something. Order the pains your product solves by how often they occur and lead with the most frequent one, then use the next one in the second email.

## Weaknesses

- **Unverified brand claims.** "Google's only recommended CRM" and "we did this for Uber and Shopify" are placeholders that read as false unless true.
- **Fake "Re:" subjects.** Two templates use a reply prefix on a first message, which the checklist already warns against.
- **Very generic bodies.** The first-contact "Loved your work" and the quick collaboration call say nothing specific.
- **Risky humor.** The "fallen and can't get up" line can land badly with a senior reader.
- **Assumes a prior request.** Several follow-ups claim the prospect "asked for details".
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A follow-up that fixes an omission ("should probably have added the link") instead of nagging.
- Ordering pains across a sequence from most to least common.
- The CCQ method as a quick research prompt.
- Two-segment A/B tests that vary the list as well as the copy.
- Subject lines matter less than the body, so keep them short.
