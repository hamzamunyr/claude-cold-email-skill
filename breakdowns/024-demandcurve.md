# Demand Curve Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Demand Curve blog "12 Cold Email Templates and Examples to Inspire Your Outreach", from a growth education company
**Template number:** 024 (one source, 12 templates analyzed; each has a filled example that was an image, and I viewed all 12 plus the final checklist image)
**Subject lines:** provided for the examples (listed below, with dashes changed to hyphens)
**Performance data:** not provided. The examples are illustrative, and the page tells readers to treat templates as inspiration, not rules.

> Note on the source: the examples are screenshots with gray redaction boxes over names. The page pairs each template with a line from a classic writing guide, which I leave out. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

SaaS, agencies, founders and creators. Only templates 1 to 3, 8 and 9 are straight sales. The rest are fundraising, content and partnership uses of the same structure.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a competitor or tool they use (3), their content (3), their company page (1), a self-aware apology (1), a prior touch (1), their own content (1).
- **CTAs:** a 15 to 20 minute call (4), a calendar link (3), an interest check (3), a direct 30 minute meeting (1), a specific time (1), a free trial (1), an introduction (1), an interview (1).
- **Personalization:** research-driven, one detail per email. The page recommends testing a low-commitment "any interest?" against sending a calendar link right away.

## Sequence and cadence

Follow-ups need to be sharper than first emails: who you are, what value, which peers, and a specific time. The page points to its own guide for how many to send and does not give a fixed number.

## Weaknesses

- **Several are long.** Templates run 100 to 120 words, above what the checklist asks.
- **Competitor attacks.** Naming a tool's limits can read as aggressive and must be accurate.
- **Gift-card incentive.** The follow-up offers a gift card for a call, which can feel like a bribe and costs money.
- **Free-work scarcity.** "Only a few spots left" is only fine if true.
- **Investment pitch claims** (the round size, "perfect match") need to be true and cannot be copied.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A meeting that pays off even if they say no ("I'll share tactics either way").
- Skipping the call with a free trial for easy-to-try products.
- Scoping a free offer like a project brief.
- Asking for the right person with an apology and a one-sentence exit.
- A final checklist of nine items: a clear reason, value, objections handled, one CTA, a de-risked meeting, a personal touch, concise, approachable, authentic.
