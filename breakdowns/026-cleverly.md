# Cleverly Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Cleverly blog "B2B Cold Sales Email Templates", from a cold email agency (updated June 2026)
**Template number:** 026 (one source, 15 emails analyzed: 8 templates, 3 follow-up templates, and 4 extra touches inside two sequences)
**Subject lines:** provided for most templates (listed below), plus a list of 7 subject patterns
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The agency claims 30%+ open rates for its subject patterns and big totals for its own business. None of it is verified or tied to a template.

> Note on the source: an agency page. All text, no email images. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The templates

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Value add, 4-touch | "Suggestions for {Company}" | A suggestion to improve two metrics, a client result, then an offer to send the ideas, a new-thread value line, a case study |
| 2 | Problem-solution | two lowercase words | A trigger, a guess, what clients usually face, a quantified result, "worth a convo?" |
| 3 | LinkedIn connection | (a connection note, not an email) | A friendly note plus a link to a high-engagement post |
| 4 | Referral | "{First Last Name} asked me to contact you" | A named referrer, a quantified result, an overview offer |
| 5 | Executive outreach | "Quick thought on [Company's] growth in [market/quarter]" | A trigger, a peer result, 15 minutes |
| 6 | Lead generation ask | "Idea for [Company's] [relevant challenge or goal]" | An observation, an outcome, a breakdown offer, plus a second touch with an exit |
| 7 | AIDA | "[Their goal] - worth a quick look?" | A hook, a problem and consequence, a client before and after, a 15 minute call |
| 8 | PAS | two lowercase words | A problem, the downstream cost, a solution without the usual tradeoff, a quick call |
| 9 | Follow-up: value add | same thread | A short case study or resource, a soft offer |
| 10 | Follow-up: honest re-engage | same thread | "Is the timing just off?", an offer to come back later, an offer to stop |
| 11 | Follow-up: breakup | "Closing the loop" | Says it will stop, leaves contact details |

The subject list also includes patterns such as a referrer's name, "Hey [first name]", "Via [first name]", a content form in a publication, and "found you through [source]".

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers and outbound agencies. The page frames templates as the vehicle and the personalized opening observation as the engine.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction | All 15 (under 100 words, follow-ups under 50) | Quick to read |
| Commitment minimization | About 13 | Small asks |
| Quantified benefit | 8 | Concrete outcome |
| Social proof and specificity | 7 each | Proof and a trigger |
| Reciprocity | 5 (ideas, a breakdown, a case study) | Gives first |
| Consistency | 5 (the follow-ups) | Continues a thread |
| Lighter ask | 4 (send a breakdown before a meeting) | Smaller first yes |
| Easy exit | 4 | A no is easy |
| Normalization | 3 ("most [job titles] we work with") | Makes the problem common |
| Risk reversal | 2 ("worst case, ignore them") | Low stakes |
| Curiosity gap | 2 (two-word subjects) | Plain and intriguing |
| Borrowed trust, problem agitation, loss aversion, authority | 1 each | Referral, PAS, cost of inaction, a high-engagement post |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a trigger event (3), a prior touch (3), a referrer, a growth or role posting, a LinkedIn profile.
- **CTAs:** a 15 minute call (4), a send-a-breakdown or overview offer (5), an interest question (2), a specific time (1), a reply either way (1), a soft close (1), a connection (1).
- **Personalization:** the opening observation. The page says the trigger line is the most important variable.

## Sequence and cadence

Day 1 first email, day 4 value add, day 9 honest re-engage, day 14 breakup. Run a template for 50 to 100 sends before changing it. The page cites a 22% reply gain from a single follow-up, which I could not verify.

## Weaknesses

- **Agency claims.** The totals for its own business are unverified.
- **Placeholder results.** Every template relies on a peer metric that must be true.
- **The connection note is a LinkedIn message,** not an email, and its tone is overly folksy.
- **Quantity rule.** "Reply rate under 3% is almost always targeting" is a rule of thumb, not data.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A lighter first ask: offer a breakdown before asking for a meeting.
- A follow-up trio where each touch adds something and the last gives a clean exit.
- An honest re-engage that offers to stop.
- Short subjects of two words, lowercase.
- The rule to change one thing at a time after 50 to 100 sends.
