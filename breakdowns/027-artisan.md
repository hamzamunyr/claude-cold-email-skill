# Artisan Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Artisan blog "15 best cold email templates for more B2B leads in 2026", from an AI sales tool vendor
**Template number:** 027 (one source, 15 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page cites a 23.9% open rate and a 1% reply rate from other sources, which I could not verify.

> Note on the source: a vendor page. Many examples are B2C or made-up brands (a dating workshop, an animal shelter, a chef academy), so the page is weaker as B2B evidence than its title suggests. No images hold emails. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

A broad mix: sellers, service providers, creators and event hosts. Only a few are true B2B cold emails. The page also teaches a six-point way to write one: a subject about 50 characters long, a personalized greeting beyond the first name, and a value proposition in the opening.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment minimization | 9 (a reply, a quick call, a calendar link) | Small asks |
| Specificity | 6 (a post, a new firm, a meetup) | Shows research |
| Effort reduction | 6 (the brief ones) | Quick to read |
| Scarcity and urgency | 5 (capped seats, "ASAP", fifty spots) | A deadline, but easy to overdo |
| Reciprocity | 4 (a free workshop, a webinar, a free campaign) | Gives first |
| Liking through compliment | 3 (their post, firm, community) | Rapport |
| Problem agitation, loss aversion, humor, quantified benefit, risk reversal, curiosity | 2 each | PAS, "you don't want to miss", emoji, alumni stats, "not selling" |
| Empathy, borrowed trust, no-ask framing, social proof | 1 each | A shared dating problem, a mutual contact, a no-pressure line, alumni results |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a milestone, their LinkedIn post, a mutual connection, a meetup, a pain question, a local cause.
- **CTAs:** a reply (4), a reserve or demo link (4), a short call (3), a calendar link (2), a right-person ask (1), a demo (1).
- **Personalization:** the page says a first name is not enough and adds a note about a recent achievement or interaction in the greeting.

## Sequence and cadence

Not given beyond "follow up" and one busy-prospect template meant for a follow-up.

## Weaknesses

- **Several are bad models.** "Trust me, you don't want to miss this" and the line about a stalled home sale blame the reader, and "once in a lifetime opportunity" is hype.
- **Unverifiable claims.** A blockchain "fraction of the cost" claim and alumni percentages have no backing.
- **Urgency stacking.** Capped seats, "act ASAP" and "doors close" appear across several templates.
- **Some are not B2B** and are weak transfer evidence for a seller.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- The "who chooses" email that asks for the right person instead of guessing.
- A question that says plainly it is not a pitch.
- A beta invite with a reply-only ask.
- Referring to a specific story from their post, then asking one question.
