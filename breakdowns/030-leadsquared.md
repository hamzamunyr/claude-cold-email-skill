# LeadSquared Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** LeadSquared blog "20 Best Cold Email Templates To Warm Their Inbox", from a CRM vendor
**Template number:** 030 (one source, 20 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page says you can send a thousand emails and get twelve replies, and cites "over 60% more leads" for blogging brands, with no source. I treat both as unverified.

> Note on the source: a vendor page. The templates are short and loose, and several are about networking, research and recommendations, not selling. The page has two decorative images and no emails in images. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

Sellers and networkers. Many templates are about starting a relationship, with the pitch left for later.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction | All 20 (very short) | Quick to read |
| Commitment minimization | About 14 (a reply, a quick answer, a favor) | Small asks |
| Specificity | About 10 (their post, report, request) | Shows research |
| Liking through compliment | 8 | Rapport |
| Reciprocity | 5 (research, introductions, a write-up) | Gives first |
| Mutual benefit | 3 (referral swaps, shared research) | Win-win |
| Social proof, quantified benefit, authority, intent signal | 2 each | A result claim, a percentage, a signal like a liked article |
| Borrowed trust, exclusivity, normalization, loss aversion, similarity, consistency, empathy | 1 each | A named referrer, results "exclusively with you", brands stop blogging |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** their LinkedIn or social post (3), a new role, a milestone, their report, a mutual connection, their blog (2), a prior engagement.
- **CTAs:** a small favor or opinion ask (6), an offer of help or an introduction (3), an interest check (3), an asset offer (2), a referral ask (2), a calendar link (1).
- **Personalization:** one research note per email. The tips say to segment lists, use an active reply address and a business domain, and test the first two or three words of the subject.

## Tips worth noting (from the page)

Start with text, not image-heavy emails, since images and buttons can send mail to spam. Test sends to Gmail and Outlook addresses first. Put a link in the first paragraph if you want clicks. Keep decks as PDFs.

## Weaknesses

- **Guilt and rumor lines.** "We miss your blog posts" and the "informal survey says your team is struggling" line judge or invent facts about the reader.
- **Unverifiable sourcing.** Talking to a prospect's employees or vendors, then citing it, can feel like surveillance.
- **Overclaims.** "I know the solution!" and "I have always been your fan" are hollow.
- **Vague asks.** Several end with "let me know" and no next step.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A favor ask for the right person.
- A survey or research invite that promises early results only to the participant.
- A congratulation with no ask.
- Offering an introduction to someone who asked publicly.
- Following up a real LinkedIn interaction by email.
