# Salesdorado Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Salesdorado "Cold Email Templates: 8 Effective Templates for 2026", from a B2B sales media site
**Template number:** 031 (one source, 8 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page cites third-party figures I could not verify: about 3% average reply rate, 344 emails per meeting, a CTA test where an interest ask booked about double the meetings of a specific time slot, and drops from pitching, ROI claims and numbers in subjects.

> Note on the source: the page argues that the trigger matters more than the template and gives a six-point grid to score any template. It is the most data-minded page in this repo, but its numbers are secondhand. It has no images with emails. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The 6-point grid (as the page states it)

1. Ask for interest, not a specific time slot.
2. Under 100 words, three to four sentences.
3. No product description in the first email.
4. A short subject with no numbers, questions or jargon.
5. No ROI promise at first contact.
6. A specific, verifiable trigger for writing today.

The page says a template that fails two or more should be binned. It admits one of its own sources recommended longer emails and says to test long copy for highly technical buyers.

## The 8 templates

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Job posting | "Open Sales Administration Position" | A posting open for weeks, a hypothesis about why, a comparable client, "does this sound familiar, or am I missing the point?" |
| 2 | New decision-maker | "Your Appointment" | A role under 90 days, a common blind spot, a similar client, "are you working on this or is it solved?" |
| 3 | Tool change | "Salesforce Migration" | Job posts for a new tool, a likely pain, a similar client, an out ("or do you already have support?") |
| 4 | Public announcement | "Madrid Office Opening" | A new office, a counterintuitive challenge, one question |
| 5 | Comparable customer | "Feedback on distribution" | A project for a similar buyer, an operational result, an offer to send changes without a meeting |
| 6 | Internal recommendation | "Forwarded by {Contact's First Name}" | A colleague named, "not sure it's on your radar", a clean out |
| 7 | No lead, honest | "Discount Approval" | Admits no good reason to write, a small-sample field fact, a real question |
| 8 | Executives | "Margin Erosion" | A public statement, a peer's drain, an option to send bullets or an intro to their CEO |

Each template is 50 to 70 words, mentions no product, and ends on a question or choice that is not a meeting.

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers who can find a real signal. The page sorts templates by trigger (a posting, a new leader, a tool, an announcement, a client result, a referral, no signal, a priority) and says where to find each signal.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction, commitment minimization, specificity | All 8 | Short, small ask, shows research |
| Easy exit | 5 ("or am I missing the point?", "I won't pursue it") | A no is easy |
| Intent signal | 5 (a posting, a new role, a tool, an announcement, a quote) | A reason to write now |
| Social proof | 5 (a comparable client) | Proof by similarity |
| Normalization | 2 ("people in this role often tell us") | Makes it shared |
| Contrarian angle, lighter ask | 2 each | "The hard part is Y, not X", a bullet offer |
| Self-aware honesty, borrowed trust, consistency, forced choice, quantified benefit | 1 each | Admitting no reason, a colleague's name |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a job posting (2), a new role, a tool change, an announcement, a public quote, a comparable result, a prior email to a colleague, an admission.
- **CTAs:** a two-answer question (3), an asset offer (3), a dialogue question (2), an interest check (2), a format choice (1), a reply-either-way (1).
- **Personalization:** each template has a "line to rewrite" the reader must make true, such as a hypothesis, a verbatim quote or a technical detail.

## Sequence and cadence

Not specific. The page says most replies come from follow-ups and links to its own guide. It advises waiting two to three weeks after a job-change alert so the email does not look automated.

## Weaknesses

- **Secondhand statistics.** All figures are cited from other sources and not checked here.
- **Rules that conflict with others.** "No questions in the subject" and "never ask for a time" differ from other sources that ask for a 15 minute call. Test both.
- **Heavy research.** Most templates need real, fresh signals and do not scale to a bulk list.
- **Admitting no reason** is a one-off. Used in every sequence it stops working.
- **No results per template.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- The six-point grid as a quick scoring tool.
- Sorting by trigger, with where to find it.
- A respectable out inside the question.
- Using a small honest sample ("six companies we looked at") over a big vague percentage.
- Asking for the wrong contact first, then citing them to the right person, only if real.
- A "line to rewrite" note for each template.
