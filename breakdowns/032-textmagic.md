# TextMagic Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** TextMagic blog "15 Brilliant cold email templates guaranteed to get you more sales", from a business texting vendor
**Template number:** 032 (one source, 15 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page says cold emails typically see 15 to 25% opens and 1 to 5% replies, with no source, so I treat that as unverified. The word "guaranteed" in the title is not backed by any result.

> Note on the source: the template bodies are in the page text. The images are only title banners ("Template #1: ..."), which I viewed. Several bodies are 120 to 170 words. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The 15 templates

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recent event | "Congratulations on your [Recent industry award]!" | Congrats on an award, a proven-record claim, a 15 minute call with a link |
| 2 | B2B value | "Helping your team accomplish [X]" | They downloaded a whitepaper, three result bullets, a calendar link |
| 3 | Find the decision-maker | "Streamline your team's productivity and reduce operating costs with [Your company name]" | Asks who runs IT, three benefits, big-name clients, a calendar or a referral ask |
| 4 | Introduction | "Resource for [industry/strategy] questions" | Introduces the sender, a website review, a named similar company, a 15 minute call |
| 5 | Free tool | "Looking to reduce your churn rate?" | A free calculator, what it outputs, a calendar link |
| 6 | Follow-up after a talk | "Let's cut to the chase, [Prospect's first name]" | Asks if they are still interested in collaborating |
| 7 | Can I send you some ideas? | "Two ways to improve your [prospect's service/product]" | An observation, two ideas, an offer to send a short video |
| 8 | Press mention | "Loved reading about the new launch of [Prospect's company name] in [Publication title]!" | Praise for a launch, an offer to discuss, a short meeting |
| 9 | Voicemail follow-up | "Sorry I couldn't contact you over the phone, [Prospect's first name]" | Says a call was missed, gives a phone number and a next-call time |
| 10 | AIDA | "Boost your [X goal] with [Your company name]" | Their LinkedIn post, a roadblock, own research link, a phone invite |
| 11 | Unresponsive lead | "Reaching out again regarding [X business need]" | Three reply options |
| 12 | Relevant contact | "Quick question - are you the right person to discuss [service/product you offer]?" | Asks if they are the right person, asks who is |
| 13 | Educational | "Discover valuable insights - our new B2B sales podcast is out" | Announces a podcast, a link, no ask |
| 14 | PAS | "Is customer service a pain point for [Prospect's company name]?" | Notes negative reviews, a CRM solution, a brief call with a link |
| 15 | Referral | "[Prospect's friend's name] suggested we should connect - Solutions for [X pain point]" | A named friend, a result claim, a website link, a call |

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers and small business owners. The page mixes first emails and follow-ups, which makes it a quick pick-by-situation list.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment minimization | About 11 (a quick call, a reply) | Small asks |
| Specificity | 6 (an award, a download, a post, a review) | Shows research |
| Effort reduction | 5 (the shorter ones) | Quick to read |
| Social proof | 4 (a named client, results) | Borrowed credibility |
| Reciprocity | 3 (a tool, a video, a podcast) | Gives first |
| Liking through compliment, consistency | 3 each | Rapport, a continued thread |
| Intent signal, easy exit | 2 each | A download or post, "who should I contact?" |
| Authority, borrowed trust, forced choice, problem agitation, curiosity gap | 1 each | Big names, a friend, a reply menu, bad reviews |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a recent event or press mention (2), a prior touch (3), a download or post, a mutual connection, a website review, a new podcast.
- **CTAs:** a short call (7), a calendar link (5), an open call or meeting ask (3), a right-person ask (2), a phone number (3), an asset or link (3), a reply menu (1).
- **Personalization:** research notes, such as an award, a download, a post or a review. The page adds that AI tools can draft templates.

## Sequence and cadence

Not specific. The follow-up tips say to space touches over a few days or weeks, use a multi-channel approach (a call, social or SMS), add value each time, and aim for 50 to 150 words. The page repeats the 30/30/50 time split of research, writing and follow-up.

## Weaknesses

- **Name-dropping.** The decision-maker template lists IBM, Microsoft and Dell, which must be real and permitted customers.
- **Stock openers.** Many start with "I hope this message finds you well".
- **Observation with no ask for permission.** The PAS email cites negative reviews, which can feel accusatory.
- **Long.** Several bodies run well over 100 words.
- **"Guaranteed" title.** No result is shown.

## What is genuinely useful here

- A recap-style voicemail email that says when you will call again.
- A reply menu with three honest options for unresponsive leads.
- A direct "are you the right person?" email with an ask for the right name.
- A free tool as the offer, with the output spelled out.
