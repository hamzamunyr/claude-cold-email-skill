# BluLeadz Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** BluLeadz blog "18 of the Best Cold Email Templates to Get Warm Leads", from an inbound marketing and sales agency
**Template number:** 034 (one source, 18 templates analyzed; all 18 were images, and I viewed every one)
**Subject lines:** not provided. Each image shows a title and a body only.
**Performance data:** not provided. The page cites one outside figure, that people spend about nine seconds on an email they subscribed to, from an email vendor's report.

> Note on the source: every email is a screenshot of a HubSpot-style template with brackets and a "First name" token. The page has GIFs and no text copy of the emails, so I read each image. Everything below is paraphrase.

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers using a CRM. Half are first emails, half are follow-ups tied to what a lead did.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction | About 15 (short bodies) | Quick to read |
| Commitment minimization | About 14 (a quick call, a 10 minute chat) | Small asks |
| Specificity | 4 (their site, content, a past demo) | Shows research |
| Reciprocity and consistency | 4 each | Resources, analysis, follow-ups |
| Social proof | 3 | "Several other businesses", a named customer |
| Intent signal, easy exit, normalization, liking through compliment | 2 each | An open or click, "who is responsible?" |
| Forced choice, similarity, mutual benefit, objection pre-emption, show the work | 1 each | A two-reply menu, a sports fan guess, a cross-link |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a prior touch (4), a website analysis (2), a tracked open or click (2), a shared connection, a content find, a re-engagement after a demo.
- **CTAs:** a short call (9), an open meeting ask (3), a dialogue question (2), a link or demo (2), a right-person ask (1), a reply menu (1), an asset offer (2).
- **Personalization:** brackets only. The page says to personalize with a name, position and company and to bring up pain points.

## Sequence and cadence

Follow up multiple times, and not only with pitches. A past event they mentioned is a good reason to write. The page gives no timing.

## Weaknesses

- **Forced commonality.** Guessing they are a sports fan and tying a pain point to it can read as fake.
- **Announcing a tracked open or click.** Two templates say they saw the open or click, which the checklist warns against.
- **Hype lines.** "Sounds too good to be true, but it is!" has no proof.
- **Meaningless claims.** "I ran an analysis of your website" must be true and specific, or it reads as bait.
- **Template-grade bodies.** Each is mostly brackets, so the quality depends on what you fill in.
- **No subject lines and no results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A "point me in the right direction" ask with an apology and a clear question.
- A reply-with-a-phrase breakup that gives two easy answers.
- A response to a concern raised on a call, with a resource.
- A re-engagement after a demo that names a similar customer.
