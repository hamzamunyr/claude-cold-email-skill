# Waalaxy Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Waalaxy blog "+25 best cold email templates B2B for successful campaigns", from a LinkedIn and email automation vendor
**Template number:** 035 (one source, 25 templates analyzed, plus one deliberately bad example the page shows)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below, emoji left out)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. Two figures inside templates ("already +1000 people have read it" and "+100,000 users") are vendor claims and unverified.

> Note on the source: the page text loads the template boxes only in the browser, so a plain text scrape misses them. I read them in a browser. A few templates are not cold emails at all (a welcome email, a thank-you, a promo code, a cart reminder). Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

Marketers, sales teams and freelancers using automation. Roughly half are real cold emails, the rest are lifecycle or relationship emails.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction | About 20 (short) | Quick to read |
| Commitment minimization | About 18 | Small asks |
| Specificity | 6 (a role, a post, a download, a city) | Shows research |
| Reciprocity | 5 (a guide, a free test, an article) | Gives first |
| Risk reversal | 4 ("no big sales pitch", "nothing formal", "free") | Lowers stakes |
| Humor | 4 (karaoke, jokes) | Stands out |
| Social proof, curiosity gap, intent signal, consistency, quantified benefit | 3 each | Counts, questions, a download |
| Similarity, liking, normalization, mutual benefit | 2 each | A shared city, congrats |
| Scarcity, no-ask framing, problem agitation, competitive comparison, easy exit, self-aware honesty | 1 each | A deadline, "not here to knock" |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a new role, a competitor they use, a download (2), a LinkedIn post, a shared city, a prior touch (2), a pain question (2), a thank-you, a welcome, their job.
- **CTAs:** an opinion or dialogue question (about 9), a link to register or try (4), a short call (3), a coffee or call (2), a calendar link, a review or recommendation ask, a reply keyword, a score request.
- **Personalization:** first name, company, job title and a recent action. The page names three levels: by first name, by job and company, and by an action.

## Sequence and cadence

The page says a follow-up should keep the same structure, change the teaser and CTA, stay under 500 characters, and avoid "did you see my message?". It suggests a pattern break with humor for the last touch, and waiting for two or three exchanges before proposing a call. It also advises combining email with LinkedIn and not overdoing frequency.

## Weaknesses

- **Many are not cold emails.** The welcome, thank-you, cart and promo templates only fit people who already bought or asked.
- **Competitor-user outreach.** Emailing a rival's users pulled from social data raises privacy and permission questions.
- **Emoji in nearly every subject.** The page itself lists spammy words but still leans on emoji.
- **Unverified numbers** in several bodies.
- **Casual closings** like "See you soon" on a first contact.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- "No big sales pitch" as a real opener for relationship-first intros.
- A humorous final follow-up built on a pattern break, with a time ask attached.
- A score-and-reason request to ask a prospect to rate your email.
- Asking for expert input, then mentioning a beta in one short line.
- A shown bad example that piles on capitals, emoji and buzzwords.
