# Clearout Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Clearout blog "22 Effective Cold Email Templates That Get Replies in 2025", from an email verification vendor
**Template number:** 036 (one source, 22 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page cites outside stats (35% of people open on the subject alone, "6x" higher transaction rates for personalized email, 26% more opens, a single CTA lifting clicks 371%) without tying them to a template. I could not verify them.

> Note on the source: a vendor page that sells list verification, so it stresses clean lists and spam words. All 22 bodies are in the page text, with a use case for each. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines (a dash changed to a hyphen).

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers, recruiters and creators. The groups are first touches, follow-ups, referrals and offers. The page says to keep emails to about 120 words, and most here are 40 to 80.

|---|---|---|

| Commitment minimization | About 20 | Small asks |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a post (2), an event, a mutual or referrer (2), a competitor, a prior touch (3), a research finding, a company initiative.
- **CTAs:** a short call (11), an interest check (3), a right-person ask (2), a demo or look (2), an open meeting ask (2), a dialogue question.
- **Personalization:** a recent accomplishment, a quote from their content, or a pain point in their area.

## Sequence and cadence

The page says to wait 4 to 5 days for the first follow-up and to end a series with a close-the-loop note. It also says to verify lists and keep spam words (free, guaranteed, limited time, capitals) out.

## Weaknesses

- **Self-contradiction.** The page warns against "limited time", then uses it in a subject line.
- **Bare follow-up.** "Just following up" and "did you see my last message?" is the weak style other sources warn against.
- **Vague filler.** "A realistic yet extraordinary idea" and "a fresh approach" name nothing.
- **Fake exclusivity.** "The select few companies" needs a real list.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A "who handles this?" email that asks for the owner.
- A short event-connection note after a shared event.
- An email that admits the last one was unclear and restates the point.
- The advice to verify the list and stay compliant before blaming the copy.
