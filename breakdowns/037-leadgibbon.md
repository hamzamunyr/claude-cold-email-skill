# Lead Gibbon Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Lead Gibbon blog "11 awesome cold email templates and why they work" (first published 2016, updated 2021), a lead generation blog
**Template number:** 037 (one source, 11 templates analyzed; every email was an image, and I viewed all 11)
**Subject lines:** provided for some templates (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page shows charts about short emails, personalization and follow-ups taken from other sites, which I could not verify.

> Note on the source: the author collected these templates from other companies' sites (the page credits several). All 11 emails are screenshots with brackets. This is older material than most of the repo, which is worth knowing. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

General B2B sellers. The page sorts them into introductions, lead finding and making a sale.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction, commitment minimization | All 11 (5 to 6 lines) | Short, small asks |
| Social proof | 4 | Clients, investors |
| Specificity | 3 (a site, a signup, a download) | Shows research |
| Quantified benefit | 3 | Lift claims |
| Curiosity gap, reciprocity, intent signal, easy exit, forced choice | 2 each | A named competitor, an offer to write up, time options |
| Borrowed trust, self-aware honesty, problem agitation, liking, consistency, authority, competitive comparison, normalization | 1 each | A named referrer, "not another pitch" |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a referrer, their site (2), a download or signup (2), a pain question (2), a peer result, a prior touch, a role pattern.
- **CTAs:** a short call (7), time options (3), a right-person ask (2), a question (2), a write-up or video (2), a longer 30 minute ask (1).
- **Personalization:** mentions of their site, signups or downloads. The page says to start with praise before pointing out a problem.

## Weaknesses

- **Fake-feeling bracket text.** Most use "numerous companies" and "key issue" with no detail.
- **Blunt subject.** "Bookkeeping sucks" is edgy and generic at once.
- **Large unproven claims.** "70% increase in contact rates" must be provable.
- **Tracking tone.** Template 7 refers to a recent visit and download, which can feel watchful.
- **Old.** Several practices and statistics date from 2016.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A transparent intro that says it is not another sales pitch and shows who backs you.
- A praise-then-fix email with an offer to write it up.
- A one-line quick follow-up that gives a time or a name to redirect to.
- The line "who is the best person to speak with about this?" at the end of a pitch.
