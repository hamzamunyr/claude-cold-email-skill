# MailGenius Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** MailGenius blog "8 Cold Email Marketing Templates That Actually Work (2026)", from an email spam-testing vendor
**Template number:** 039 (one source, 8 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page quotes outside figures that I could not verify: 6 to 8 sentence emails averaging a 42.67% open rate and 6.9% reply rate, a test where clearer wording doubled replies and gave 97% more appointments, 15 to 25% typical opens and 1 to 8.5% replies, and a rise from 9% to 13% replies with one follow-up and up to 27% with two or three.

> Note on the source: a vendor page that argues deliverability matters as much as copy. Most images are stock photos with a template name on them. One image shows a phone with a very bare follow-up ("just checking in to see if you had a chance to review the proposal"), used as an example of weak copy, and I counted it as a negative illustration only. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The 8 templates

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem, agitate, solve | "Reducing no-show demos at {{Company}}" | Noticed growth, the common wall at that stage, a narrow fix, "worth a quick look?" |
| 2 | Pattern interrupt | "Should I leave this alone?" | Admits it was going to send the usual pitch, a real observation, "open to seeing the idea?" |
| 3 | One-liner | "Idea for {{Company}} onboarding" | A trigger, one value line, an offer to send the short version |
| 4 | Social proof | "Similar result for {{ProspectCompany}}?" | A similar team's fix, why they look alike, an offer to send a breakdown |
| 5 | Referral | "{{ReferrerName}} suggested I reach out" | A named referrer, their initiative, an offer of a quick idea |
| 6 | Value-first | "One thing I'd fix in {{Company}}'s {{channel}}" | An observation, a simple fix, an offer of two more ideas |
| 7 | AIDA | "{{specific result area}} at {{Company}}" | An observation, an industry issue, a simplified process, a brief chat next week |
| 8 | Curiosity gap | "Noticed something on {{Company}}'s funnel" | One issue spotted, "it's fixable", an offer of the short version |

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers, agencies and SaaS teams. The page treats each template as a draft to test for spam signals before sending.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction and commitment minimization | All 8 (about 25 to 70 words) | Quick to read, small asks |
| Specificity | 6 (a trigger, an observation, a funnel) | Shows research |
| Lighter ask | 6 (an offer to send an idea or the short version) | A smaller first yes |
| Curiosity gap | 3 | Hints without giving it all away |
| Normalization, reciprocity, intent signal | 2 each | "A lot of teams hit that wall" |
| Social proof, self-aware honesty, contrarian angle, borrowed trust, problem agitation, easy exit | 1 each | A similar team, "waste of your time", a named referrer |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a trigger event (2), a real observation (2), a referrer, a self-aware admission, a similar team.
- **CTAs:** permission to send an idea or the short version (5), a short asset offer, an interest check (2), a brief chat (1).
- **Personalization:** one specific observation. The page warns that a fake or loose referral makes the sender look risky.

## Sequence and cadence

The page says a template belongs inside a sequence with clean, low-pressure follow-ups. It cites a gain from at least one follow-up and a larger one from two or three, which I could not verify. It advises no calendar link in a first touch.

## Weaknesses

- **Thin bodies.** Every example is mostly brackets, so quality depends on the specifics you add.
- **Unverified statistics** from third parties.
- **A "pattern interrupt" can feel gimmicky** if it admits to being a template and the next line is not strong.
- **Vendor framing.** The page ends on its own spam test.
- **No results per template.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- Offering to send the short version instead of asking for a meeting.
- Treating deliverability as part of the template: few links, plain formatting, no hype words, authentication set up.
- Using a referral only when the connection is real and named with context.
- Giving the value inside the email before asking for a click.
