**Source:** Influno blog "9 Best Cold Email Templates for SEO Outreach that Get Replies", from an SEO outreach tool vendor
**Template number:** 041 (one source, 9 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below, dashes changed to hyphens)
**Performance data:** not provided per template. The page shows a chart of average response rates by email type that I read in the browser: introductory emails 1 to 5%, content-focused 2 to 10%, sales pitch 1 to 3%, networking 5 to 15%, feedback or survey requests 10 to 30%, guest posting 5 to 15%, and link building 2 to 8%. | # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personalized link pitch | "[First Name], quick note about your [Topic] article" | Names their article and a section, offers a guide, offers anchor text or a summary |
| 2 | Guest post pitch | "[First Name], article idea for [Website Name]" | Praise for a post, a proposed title, a summary, an outline offer |

## Who it targets and why it fits

SEO and content people asking site owners to add a link, publish a guest post or co-create content. The pattern also fits anyone suggesting a resource to someone who wrote about a topic, so the structure transfers beyond SEO.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Effort reduction, commitment minimization, specificity | All 9 (each references their article or site) | Short, small ask, shows research |
| Reciprocity | 8 (a resource, an article, a suggested sentence) | Gives something useful |
| Lighter ask | 6 (a summary, an outline, anchor text) | A smaller first yes |
| Liking through compliment | 4 | Rapport |
| Mutual benefit | 3 (guest post, partnership, link) | Win-win |
| Show the work, social proof, borrowed trust, curiosity gap, consistency, easy exit | 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 | An outline, an earlier feature |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** their article or site (8), a mutual contact, a prior email, a content gap.
- **CTAs:** a link inclusion request (4), an offer to send a summary or outline (5), an interest check (2), a dialogue question.
- **Personalization:** the article title and a specific section, plus the site name. The page says to personalize every email and avoid sounding automated.

## Sequence and cadence

Plan two or three follow-ups over a couple of weeks: the first a quick reminder 4 to 7 days later, the second adding a new angle or resource. Send midweek mornings in the recipient's time zone (Tuesday to Thursday, 9 to 12). Use a separate, warmed domain for cold outreach so the main domain's reputation is protected.

## Weaknesses

- **Link-building bias.** Most templates ask for a link, so they only transfer where you offer something relevant to a specific page.
- **Bare follow-up.** "Just following up on my last note" repeats the weak pattern other sources warn against.
- **Referral wording.** Template 4 says a mutual contact "mentioned your site", which is only honest if true.
- **Vague asks** such as "let me know what you think".
- **Vendor framing.** The FAQ pushes the vendor's own AI personalization.
- **No results per template.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- Naming the exact article and section, and offering the anchor text or summary so the reader does almost no work.
- A guest post pitch built on a specific title and a short outline offer.
- A content-gap email that offers one useful sentence.
- A separate, warmed sending domain and midweek timing.
