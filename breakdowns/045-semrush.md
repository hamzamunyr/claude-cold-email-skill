# Semrush Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Semrush blog "11 Cold Email Templates Sure to Attract Hot Leads" (October 2023), from an SEO and marketing software vendor
**Template number:** 045 (one source, 13 emails analyzed: 11 templates plus 2 real example emails shown as images)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided. The page gives no results for any template.

> Note on the source: a vendor page where each template is paired with an SEO tool tutorial. The template images repeat the text, and I viewed two. I also viewed the two real email screenshots with names blurred: a weak, impersonal guest-post pitch used as a bad example, and a short broken-link email used to show a clear call to action. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

SEO, content and PR people asking for links, features or reviews, plus a general sales email and a follow-up. The principles transfer to any request where you offer something in return.

## Psychology used

| Trigger | Where it shows up | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment minimization | About 11 | A single small ask |
| Reciprocity | 8 (a finished article, a share, a guide, ideas, a reward) | Gives something |
| Specificity, liking through compliment | 6 each | A keyword, an article, past work |
| Mutual benefit | 5 | A trade of value |
| Effort reduction | 6 (several are long) | Short ones read fast |
| Social proof | 3 | Other work, a past collaboration |
| Quantified benefit, authority | 2 each | Search volume, a research report |
| Competitive comparison, easy exit, exclusivity, consistency | 1 each | A rival ranks, "tell me when to reach back out" |

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** their article or content (3), a competitor's ranking, their past collaboration, a mention of your brand, a research finding, a fan note.
- **CTAs:** an interest check (5), a link inclusion request (3), a feature request (2), an offer to send a guide or report (2), a short call, a testimonial request, a reply to join.
- **Personalization:** the page says to research pain points, address them by name, and make each email unique. Its bad example shows what happens when you do not.

## Sequence and cadence

Wait three to five business days between messages and stop at three follow-ups. Change the message each time, and let follow-ups get shorter.

## Weaknesses

- **Several templates are long** and read as essays.
- **"Is it fair to say you struggle with X?"** assumes a pain it cannot know.
- **"Hey There"** and a long bio in the bad example are shown as errors, but a few templates still open with "I hope you are doing great".
- **A "Re:" follow-up subject** is fine in the same thread and wrong on a new message.
- **"Exclusive" in a subject line** is a spam-trigger pattern.
- **Vendor framing.** Most tips are tool tutorials.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- Handing over anchor text so the reader does nothing but paste.
- A finished article offer with a credit-only ask.
- A testimonial request that offers guiding questions and says exactly how to submit.
- A broken-link email whose ask is one short line.
- A reminder to make the benefit to the recipient explicit, such as sharing their article in return.
