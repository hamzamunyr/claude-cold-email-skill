# Maildoso Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Maildoso blog "Best B2B Cold Email Templates for Strategic Outreach", from a cold email infrastructure vendor
**Template number:** 047 (one source, 15 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided as real results. Each template says the sender improved a client by a percentage (15 to 40%), which are placeholders written as claims. I treat all of them as unverified.

> Note on the source: a vendor page whose main pitch is mailboxes and deliverability. Each template lists a target, a trigger, a subject, a body and a "why it works" note. Several notes contain leftover phrases such as "unique from your ..." that look like editing leftovers from a prompt. The images are decorative. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B sellers to named roles, each tied to a public trigger. The page maps each template to AIDA, PAS, BAB, SPIN, storytelling or a value canvas.

## Psychology used

All 15 use effort reduction, commitment minimization and specificity (a stated target and trigger). Social proof and quantified benefit appear in about 14 (a client result). An intent signal appears in about 12 (a report, funding, a post, a visit, an event). Mutual benefit (2), borrowed trust (1) and reciprocity (1) also appear.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a trigger event (6), a LinkedIn or X post (2), a session or webinar (2), a mutual contact, a website visit, a forum comment, an award.
- **CTAs:** every template asks for one specific day and time ("Monday at 11 AM"), plus one asks to reply "send".
- **Personalization:** a named role, a size band and one public trigger per template.

## Sequence and cadence

Several follow-ups a few days apart, varied in content. Little else is given.

## Weaknesses

- **A single fixed time to a stranger.** It assumes they are free and reads as pushy. Offer two options or a short range.
- **Unverified percentages** in every body.
- **Tracking visits.** "Saw you on our blog" can feel like surveillance.
- **Vendor framing and editing leftovers** in the "why it works" notes.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A target-and-trigger line for each template, which is a good prompt for research.
- A resource offer with a one-word reply option beside a call.
- Matching the framework to the trigger (a report to PAS, a funding round to BAB).
