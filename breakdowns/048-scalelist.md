# Scalelist Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Scalelist "Cold email templates for B2B: 16 first-touch examples", from a lead data vendor
**Template number:** 048 (one source, 16 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for the templates, and the page says so: "We do not publish a reply rate for the templates on this page." It lists published averages from other sources with their denominators (0.45% per email sent, 3.43% per email sent with the top 10% at 10.7% or more, and 4.5% per recipient), and Hunter figures such as higher replies without open tracking, from custom domains and from small sends. I could not verify them.

> Note on the source: the clearest first-touch page in this repo. Each template is under 80 words, uses fictional sellers (a scheduling tool, staffing software, backup, health scheduling, sales onboarding), and explains why it works and when to use it. The page has four diagrams (a picker, an annotated template, a benchmark chart and a bad-versus-good draft) that loaded blank in my scraper. The body text carries all the templates. I did not view the diagrams. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The 16 templates

| # | Group | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hiring trigger | "second shift at Northwind" | A public job post, what a new shift usually causes, a 15 minute call |

## The page's rules

Four parts in under 80 words: why them, why it matters, one proof or idea, one easy ask. Subjects are 2 to 5 words about their topic. Never put "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a first email. Send plain text to a verified address. Include a physical address and an opt-out as the law requires. Count replies per 100 recipients, per template, on similar lists, and change one thing at a time.

## Psychology used

All 16 use effort reduction and commitment minimization. Specificity appears in about 14, intent signals (a job post, a start date, a new site) in 8, a lighter ask in 8, easy exit in 8 ("or is it too early?"), normalization in 6, forced choice in 5, reciprocity in 4, curiosity in 2, and no-ask framing in 3.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a job post (4), a new leader, a new location, a system change, a role pattern (5), an industry-level fact.
- **CTAs:** a topic question (10), a short call (6), an asset offer (5), a two-answer question (3), a right-person ask (2).
- **Personalization:** one dated, public fact per email. The page says to build the list around one trigger so one template stays specific for hundreds of people.

## Weaknesses

- **Fictional sellers**, so the specifics are illustrations.
- **Vendor framing.** Scalelist's own list tool is pitched at the end.
- **Some templates assume a fact** such as a plant's overtime pattern, so use them only with a real signal.
- **Cited statistics** are from other sources and unverified here.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The "or is it too early?" and "or already handled?" closes that make a "not yet" an easy reply.
- Offering a resource and keeping the first email link-free so the reply is the ask.
- Never mentioning patient details in a cold email.
- Writing a different angle for each role in the same company, and not sending both on the same day.
- The advice to measure replies per 100 recipients, not against a single published number.
