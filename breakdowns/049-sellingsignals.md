# Selling Signals Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Selling Signals "Best Cold Email Template + 12 Templates for Any Scenario" (March 2023, updated November 2025), a sales and marketing publication
**Template number:** 049 (one source, 12 templates analyzed)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below), plus a "from line" for most
**Performance data:** not provided. The page says cold emails average about a 1% success rate with no source.

> Note on the source: a site-fetching tool could not load this page, so I read it in an ordinary browser, where it loaded with no challenge to solve. The templates are long (130 to 170 words) and each ends with a name block and a phone number. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## Who it targets and why it fits

B2B and B2C sellers. The page groups templates into direct outreach, leads who took an action, a first follow-up and a final follow-up, and recommends a from line like "Name from Company" under 50 characters.

## Psychology used

Commitment minimization appears in all 12 (a five to ten minute call), but effort reduction in only about 4 because the bodies are long. Social proof appears in 6, specificity in 6, normalization in 5 ("a lot of [titles] have been coming to us"), consistency in 5, problem agitation in 4, quantified benefit in 4, reciprocity in 3, humor in 3, intent signal in 3, and scarcity in 2.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a role or responsibility they hold, a LinkedIn post, a download, a website visit, a missed call, a prior email.
- **CTAs:** a short call (10), a phone number (9), a scheduler link (8), a reply "yes" (1).
- **Personalization:** one researched line and a role. The page says never to repeat the first email in a follow-up and to add an article, a statistic or humor.

## Sequence and cadence

A first email, a first follow-up that adds information, and a final follow-up with urgency, humor or a pattern break. No timing.

## Weaknesses

- **Long.** The bodies repeat claims and spend sentences on the sender.
- **A phone number and a scheduler in almost every email**, which makes the ask a menu.
- **Pain-first subjects** like "say goodbye to" and "sick of" are hype and mild blame.
- **Fake urgency and guilt** in the final B2B email ("downright rude").
- **Watching language.** Template 7 refers to the page they viewed.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- The "thanks for [action]" email that gives a point of contact first and mentions a related offer second.
- A first follow-up that adds an article and a statistic instead of repeating.
- A final follow-up that changes style and shows a human.
- The from-line advice: a real name plus company, not a clever title.
