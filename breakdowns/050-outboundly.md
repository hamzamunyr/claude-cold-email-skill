# Outboundly Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Outboundly blog "10 High-Converting Cold Email Templates to Boost Your Outreach Success" (August 2023, updated January 2025), from an AI outreach vendor
**Template number:** 050 (one source, 6 templates analyzed; the title says 10)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided for any template. The page gives benchmark ranges (15 to 30% opens, 5 to 10% replies, 2 to 5% conversion) with no source.

> Note on the source: the title promises ten templates, and the page has six (two sales, one networking, one follow-up, one partnership, one enterprise). The images are decorative. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## The 6 templates

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product introduction | "Helping [company name] with [specific pain point]" | A noticed challenge, a result for a similar company, a quick call |
| 2 | SaaS sales | "Save [specific percentage] on [specific task]" | A time-consuming task, a savings claim, a call |
| 3 | LinkedIn networking | "Inspired by your work at [company name]" | Their profile, an insight, a quick chat |
| 4 | Polite reminder | "Following up on my previous email" | A reminder, an offer to resend, a call |
| 5 | Collaboration | "Let's collaborate on [specific topic]" | Admiration, a project idea, a mutual goal |
| 6 | Enterprise outreach | "Helping [enterprise company name] scale [specific process]" | Acknowledges scale, a similar organization, a call |

## Psychology used

Effort reduction and commitment minimization in all 6, social proof in 4 (a similar company), specificity in 3, liking through compliment in 2, quantified benefit in 2, mutual benefit and consistency in 1 each.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a noticed challenge, their LinkedIn profile, their company page, a prior email, a past project.
- **CTAs:** a quick call (4) and an open question (2).
- **Personalization:** the page says to use a name, a company and a pain point, and to keep emails between 50 and 125 words.

## Sequence and cadence

Two to three follow-ups spaced three to five days apart, referring to the earlier email without repeating it. Midweek mornings (Tuesday to Thursday, 9 to 11) or early afternoons are suggested. A/B test subjects and CTAs.

## Weaknesses

- **Generic and thin.** Most bodies are mostly brackets.
- **Missing templates.** Six, not ten.
- **A bare follow-up** ("Following up on my previous email") is the weak style other sources avoid.
- **Vendor tool list** takes up much of the page.
- **No results.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- A tidy list of mistakes to avoid: generic emails, overloading, weak CTAs, no follow-up, spam triggers, no proofreading.
- The advice to put one main point in the email and save details for follow-ups.
