# FirstSales (72 Templates) Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** FirstSales blog "72 Cold Email Templates That Just Work in 2026" (January 2026), from a sales tool vendor. This is a different page from the earlier FirstSales source (#008).
**Template number:** 052 (one source, 72 templates analyzed; the page text was pasted into the chat and was cut off after the template section and part of the SEO advice, so I read the 72 templates and most of the advice, but not the end of the page)
**Subject lines:** provided for every template (examples listed below)
**Performance data:** not provided per template. The page quotes many statistics (a 3.43% average reply rate "from 3.43 billion emails", open rates by day, "61% of buyers use AI answer engines", reply rates by industry and role). I could not verify any of them, and some contradict each other.

> Note on the source: no images were available, because I read pasted text. All 72 bodies are very short (about 35 to 55 words) and end with a 10 to 15 minute call ask. The big concern is below. Everything below is paraphrase except subject lines.

## How the 72 are grouped

| Group | Templates | Example subject lines | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| By role | 1 to 18 | "Your team's hiring 2 more SDRs", "Your CAC increased 22%", "Net revenue retention at 85%", "Your SDR turnover is 60%" | A named metric about the prospect, a gap to an industry average, a peer's result, a 10 minute call |
| By industry | 19 to 36 | "3% trial-to-paid conversion", "68% cart abandonment", "Realization rate at 70%", "Lead time at 12 weeks" | The same pattern with an industry metric |
| By use case | 37 to 54 | "[Company]'s [specific project]", "Congrats on the [funding/launch/hire]", "Closing the loop", "See you at [Event]?", "[Mutual] suggested I reach out" | First touch, follow-ups, breakups, re-engagement, events, referrals, a competitor evaluation |
| By framework | 55 to 72 | "[Problem] killing your [metric]?", "From [current] to [desired]", "Resource for [problem]", "How [Client] hit [result]", "Who owns [responsibility] at [Company]?" | PAS, BAB, AIDA, value-first, social proof, question-based |

## The main problem: invented facts about the prospect

Most of the first 36 templates open with a specific number about the recipient and a source in brackets, such as "(checked your earnings call)", "(from Glassdoor reviews)", "(per G2 reviews)", "(based on LinkedIn activity)" or "(Facebook Ads Library data)". Those figures (a quota attainment, a CAC, a turnover rate, a sales cycle) are mostly not available from the sources named, so a sender filling the brackets would be inventing or guessing facts about a stranger. That is risky, hard to verify and creepy when it is wrong. Treat it as a pattern to avoid.

## Psychology used

All 72 use effort reduction and commitment minimization. Quantified benefit appears in about 66, social proof ("[Client] went from X to Y") in about 62, specificity in about 60, a peer benchmark comparison in about 20, an intent signal in about 30, problem agitation in 14, loss aversion in 12, curiosity in 6, easy exit in 5, borrowed trust in 4, and reciprocity in 4. These counts are approximate.

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hooks:** a stated metric about them (about 28, risky), a trigger event (12), a job post (3), a mutual or customer referral (2), an event (3), a re-engagement (3), a prior email (3), a competitor or tool (2).
- **CTAs:** a 10 to 15 minute call (about 60), an interest check (8), a dialogue question (6), an asset offer (5), a right-person ask (3), a single fixed day (6).
- **Personalization:** three parts: the observation, the problem and a relevant proof. The page calls this a two minute edit per email.

## Sequence and cadence

A cadence of day 0, day 3 and day 7, with a breakup on day 14, and a 3-touch sequence preferred over longer. The page also claims 58% of replies come from the first email and recommends emails under 80 words, with evenings sometimes beating mornings. These conflict with other sources here and are unverified.

## Weaknesses

- **Fabricated specifics** as above.
- **Unsourced industry averages** used to shame the reader ("industry average is 22%").
- **A "Re:" subject on a first email** (template 4) is the fake-reply trick other sources warn against.
- **Bold unprovable claims** such as a sentence that "sounds fake, right?"
- **Sample repetition.** 72 templates share one shape, so quantity adds little.
- **Vendor pitch** for SEO content at length.
- **No results per template.**

## What is genuinely useful here

- The three-part personalization rule: an observation, a problem in their words, and a relevant proof.
- The "who owns this?" email as an opening move.
- A re-engagement that references what they said before.
- A referral breakup that asks who else owns the problem.
- The 3-second test: the first line should be a specific observation, not an introduction.
