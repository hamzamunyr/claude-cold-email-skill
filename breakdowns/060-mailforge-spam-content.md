# Mailforge "Cold Email Content Mistakes" Breakdown

**Source:** Mailforge blog "Cold Email Content Mistakes That Trigger Spam Filters" (September 2026), from an email infrastructure vendor
**Type:** practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page quotes several statistics (for example a rise in spam flagging from stacked promotional phrases, personalization lifts). I could not verify them, so I do not reuse the numbers.

## The ten content mistakes

1. Spam trigger words and phrases ("free", "guaranteed", "act now", "congratulations"), especially several together.
2. Misleading or clickbait subject lines, including a fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" prefix.
3. Overly promotional or salesy language.
4. No personalization or relevance (cookie-cutter templates are easy for filters to spot).
5. Poor writing, typos and formatting; keep it under about 120 words with short sentences.
6. Excessive punctuation and all caps; keep subject punctuation to a minimum.
7. Too many links, attachments or images; use at most 1 or 2 full, branded links, no URL shorteners, mostly text.
8. Missing or hidden unsubscribe.
9. Poor sender identity and inconsistent from names.
10. Not testing before sending.

## Practices extracted

- Replace pushy phrases with plain, specific ones; read the email aloud, and if it sounds stiff or robotic, rewrite it.
- Subject must match the body; never fake a reply or forward.
- One call to action, no attachments, self-contained text.
- Run a spam or deliverability test and a human read before sending.

## Weaknesses

- Quoted statistics and a case anecdote are unsourced.
- Word-list thinking is partly outdated; filters judge overall patterns (ZoomInfo and Martal say this).
- Promotes its own tools.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The swap guide from hype words to plain ones.
- The "no fake Re:" rule stated clearly.
- The read-aloud test.
