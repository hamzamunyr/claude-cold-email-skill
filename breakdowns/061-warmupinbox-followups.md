# Warmup Inbox Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** Warmup Inbox blog "Cold Email Follow-Up" guide, from an email warm-up tool vendor
**Source number:** 061 (follow-up source, 10 emails counted)
**Performance data:** The page quotes several statistics (80% of sales need five follow-ups, 48% of reps never follow up, 4 to 7 emails getting 3x the replies). I could not verify them, and they conflict with its own advice of 2 to 3 follow-ups.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gentle reminder | "Just Checking In" | Circle back, no pressure, offer to help |
| 2 | Value reminder | "Exclusive Resource Just for You" | Remind them of a resource and its benefit |
| 3 | Mutual benefit | "Don't Miss Out on This Win-Win Opportunity!" | Collaboration, a stated outcome (hype subject) |
| 4 | Social proof | "Success Stories from [Industry]" | Offer stories from a shared industry |
| 5 | Limited-time offer | "Time is Running Out! Last Chance to/for [Special Offer]" | A deadline, one last reminder (fake-urgency risk) |
| 6 | Follow-up call | "Quick Chat Tomorrow?" | Ask for a call the next day |
| 7 | Personalized question | "Quick Question for You" | A custom question about their business |
| 8 | Additional resource | "Helpful Resource for You" | Share a resource tied to their interest |
| 9 | Friendly check-in | "How Are Things Going?" | Ask how the week is going, no rush |
| 10 | Solution-oriented | "Addressing Your Needs" | Tie the product to a pain at their company |

## Cadence and timing

A sample structure: follow-up 1 after 2 to 3 days (gentle reminder plus small value), follow-up 2 after 4 to 6 days (another pain point and social proof), follow-up 3 after 7 to 10 days (soft breakup), then 14 to 30 days later a nurture email with a resource. Also: wait 3 to 7 days before the first follow-up.

## Weaknesses

- Several templates assume a past conversation (when we spoke) that a cold prospect never had.
- The subject lines mix hype (Don't Miss Out, Time is Running Out!) with plain ones.
- Template 5 uses a limited-time offer that must be real.
- It sits next to a vendor pitch for a warm-up tool.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The follow-up structure table: each touch has a different job.
- The why no response checklist: self-centered, too long, no trust, typos, wrong timing, wrong list.
- The rule that a follow-up must give a reason the message matters now.
