# Artisan Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** Artisan blog "Cold Email Follow-Up" guide, from an AI sales tool vendor
**Source number:** 062 (follow-up source, 4 emails counted)
**Performance data:** It quotes figures (a 65.8% response lift from a follow-up, a 49% reply lift for the first follow-up, "interest" CTAs getting double the replies). All are unverified, and the CTA figure is attributed to Gong research.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Just checking in (first follow-up) | "Quick check-in" | Restate the reason, one line of proof, Would you like to learn more? |
| 2 | Second offer | "Interested in [alternative pain point]" | A different pain point and a result for a client in their industry, offer a case study |
| 3 | Friendly reminder with a reason | "Want to book something in?" | A real capacity limit as the urgency, a closed ask |
| 4 | Last chance | "I hate breakups" | One last email, I won't contact you about it again, hit reply for more info |

## Cadence and timing

Four-step framework: initial email, a gentle reminder follow-up, a second follow-up with an alternative offer and proof, then a final goodbye. Send the first follow-up about 3 days later; weekdays Tuesday and Thursday, 9 to 12 local time. It says 2 to 3 follow-ups is the ideal range and that gains drop after that.

## Weaknesses

- The I hate breakups subject and ciao, adios close are jokey and slightly guilt-flavored.
- Opening lines like I know you're super busy are filler.
- Heavy pitch for its own AI product.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The idea that the second follow-up should change the offer or the proof, because the first pain point may be wrong.
- The reason-for-urgency rule: name why the deadline exists.
- Sample empathy lines that acknowledge a real company event.
