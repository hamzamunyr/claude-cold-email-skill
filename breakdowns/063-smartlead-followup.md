# Smartlead Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** Smartlead blog "Cold Email Follow-Up", from a cold email software vendor
**Source number:** 063 (follow-up source, 7 emails counted)
**Performance data:** Not provided for the templates. Studies are mentioned in general terms only.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Simple reminder | "Quick follow-up on my previous email" | Refer to last week's email, ask for thoughts |
| 2 | Offer more information | "Thought this might interest you" | More detail on a benefit, a client example and result |
| 3 | Ask a question | "Quick question for you" | How are you handling [pain point]? |
| 4 | Breakup | "Should I close your file?" | Tried a few times, tell me if not interested, I will close your file |
| 5 | New information | "Thought this might be useful for you" | An article, case study or news item tied to the conversation |
| 6 | Case study | "How we helped [client name] achieve [specific result]" | A short success story from a similar client |
| 7 | Offer | "Exclusive offer for you" | A discount or trial for a limited time |

## Cadence and timing

Two to three follow-ups for cold leads. First follow-up 2 to 3 days after the first email, second 4 to 5 days later, third about 7 days after that. After three, switch channel (LinkedIn, phone). Use different subject lines across the sequence and keep subjects to 5 to 7 words.

## Weaknesses

- Template 1 asks only for thoughts, which is the bump style that other sources say is weak.
- Template 7 relies on an exclusive limited-time offer.
- A lot of the page is a feature tour of the vendor tool.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The three-follow-up timing with a stop rule and a channel switch.
- The advice to vary subject lines rather than reuse one.
- The question follow-up that asks how they handle the pain today.
