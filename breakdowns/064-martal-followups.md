# Martal Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** Martal blog "Mastering Cold Email Follow Up: How Many Touches, What to Say, and When to Stop" (2026), from an outbound agency
**Source number:** 064 (follow-up source, 9 emails counted)
**Performance data:** The page cites third-party research for its numbers (a sales research firm, Gong analyses of about 304,000 follow-up emails and 28 million cold emails, a social vs email response study). I could not verify any of them, so I record them as "reported by the source".

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No response after the first email | same thread | Open with one new, real event tied to the first email; ask if it is worth fifteen minutes |
| 2 | Follow-up after a cold call | "The [specific problem] question I called about" | Brief mention of the call, one-line problem and peer fix, offer two paragraphs |
| 3 | After a discovery call | "[Company] + [Your company]: next step" | Three things I heard, invite correction, one dated next step |
| 4 | Post-event or webinar | "Your question about [topic] at [event]" | Answer the question they asked, offer the longer version |
| 5 | Prospect goes dark | same thread | Names three reasons (priorities moved, an internal concern, timing) and asks which |
| 6 | Trigger event | "[Specific trigger]" | The event is the whole reason; too early or worth a short call? |
| 7 | Post-demo feedback | "The [feature/workflow] question from your demo" | One thing I could not tell, what did the rest of the team say? |
| 8 | Wrong person | same thread | I may have aimed this at the wrong person, point me to the owner |
| 9 | Break-up | same thread | I'll stop here, reply if it moves up the list, good luck with a real initiative |

## Cadence and timing

Three to four follow-ups after the first email (a 4 to 5 email sequence). First follow-up 2 to 3 business days later, then 4 to 7 business days, break-up about 14 days after the last touch. Spacing grows with seniority: SMB owner every 3 to 5 days, mid-market every 4 to 7, enterprise every 7 to 10, C-suite every 10 to 14. A six-touch example mixes email with a phone or LinkedIn touch on day 5 to 7 and day 14 to 18. Afternoon sends are said to beat mornings for replies (open data is unreliable).

## Weaknesses

- Several figures are secondary and unverified.
- The first template needs a genuinely new event; if you have none, the page says to wait.
- It is an agency voice, with links to its own services.

## What is genuinely useful here

- Five steps: decide what this email adds, keep the thread unless the angle changed, put the news in the first line, give one reason, ask one question that can be answered in a word.
- Reported phrase effects: Hope all is well helps, Following up hurts a little, Never heard back and Thoughts? hurt, bump-only emails book far fewer meetings.
- Follow-ups can run a little longer than the first email when the added length is real proof; under 100 words as a guide.
- The prospect goes dark email that turns silence into a multiple-choice question.
- The wrong-person email as the cheapest ask.
