# Jacob Tuwiner Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** Jacob Tuwiner blog "Best Cold Email Follow-Up Sequence (Sales)", from an outbound consultant
**Source number:** 068 (follow-up source, 3 emails counted)
**Performance data:** The author claims reply and meeting rates well above average (20% and 7 to 10%). These are self-reported and I could not verify them. The page also cites a CRM-vendor study that 4 to 7 follow-ups get 3 times the replies of 1 to 3.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bump | same thread | A short reply asking if the last message got buried, or just Bump |
| 2 | Big win | same thread | Another win with a similar company and its revenue result, ask when they are free |
| 3 | Breakup | same thread | Seems like [goal] isn't a priority this year. If that changes, let me know or Have you given up on this? |

## Cadence and timing

Follow-up 1 after 2 days, follow-up 2 after 4 more, follow-up 3 after 5 more, follow-up 4 after 7 or 8 more, then one email a month. Send at different times of the day each touch. Four follow-ups, five emails total.

## Weaknesses

- The bump email is the pattern Gong data (reported in the Martal source) says books far fewer meetings.
- Have you given up on this? is a guilt line.
- A fear-of-loss story told in crude terms. The claim that the breakup yields almost all replies is unverified.
- Claims about reply rates are self-reported.

## What is genuinely useful here

- Sending every follow-up as a reply in the same thread.
- Different send times across the sequence.
- Targeting companies of a mid-sized range where deal size and speed are both good.
