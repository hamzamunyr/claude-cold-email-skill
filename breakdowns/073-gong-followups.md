# Gong Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** Gong blog "6 Cold Email Follow-Ups that Speed Up Deals" (2021, updated 2026), from a revenue intelligence vendor
**Source number:** 073 (follow-up source, 6 emails counted)
**Performance data:** Not provided in the text I read. The page says longer follow-ups perform much better in its data and an interest-based ask doubles replies; I could not verify either, and Martal reports a different Gong finding on length.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold outreach follow-up | "Reuse email #1 subject line" | A customer's old process vs. a new one and the outcome, one line about the company, would [outcome] help your team deliver on [initiative]? (an interest ask, not a meeting ask) |
| 2 | No-show | "Missed you on the demo. Let's reschedule" | Sorry we missed [day], remind them of their pain and goal, a 30-minute block link |
| 3 | Post-call summary | "[SELLER COMPANY] Call Summary" | Top three benefits with a pain, next steps with an owner, one resource per attendee; send one email per participant |
| 4 | Loop in the decision maker | "[YOUR COMPANY] - [COMPANY NAME] executive presentation" | Team feedback has been positive, we are preparing an executive presentation and would like you in it, a date and time |
| 5 | Proposal nudge to a champion | "RE: [YOUR COMPANY] Proposal" | Just want to make sure you saw this; have you heard back from the decision maker on a sync? |
| 6 | Referral after a win | "Thank you!" | Thank them for their business, ask to be introduced to a named contact at a prospect company |

## Cadence and timing

Not a cadence guide. It stresses selling the conversation, not the meeting: ask for small yeses (interest) before asking for time.

## Weaknesses

- Mostly later-stage emails, not cold ones.
- The proposal nudge is short and vague (just want to make sure you saw this).
- The referral email uses their connection data and a named contact, which must be real.
- No data per email is shown.

## What is genuinely useful here

- One recap per participant with their own next step.
- Use their timeline, not yours, for urgency.
- Loop the decision maker in with a clear reason the meeting serves their priorities.
- Ask for a referral only after a clear win.
