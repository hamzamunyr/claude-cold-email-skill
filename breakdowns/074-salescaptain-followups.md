# SalesCaptain Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** SalesCaptain blog "Cold Email Follow Up: Proven Templates, Timing, and Tactics to 2x Your Replies", from a GTM agency
**Source number:** 074 (follow-up source, 5 emails counted)
**Performance data:** Quotes benchmarks (an 8.5% average reply rate, a one-day follow-up cutting replies by 11% and waiting 3 days adding up to 31%), citing others. Unverified.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gentle nudge after no reply | "quick follow-up" | A short reminder with context |
| 2 | Extra value | "this might help" | A resource or insight, no repeated pitch |
| 3 | Direct question | "worth a quick chat?" | One specific yes or no question |
| 4 | Breakup | "should I close this out?" | A polite last message with an easy out |
| 5 | Last follow-up before closing the file | "last try on my end" | A final value line and a clear stop |

## Cadence and timing

Day 1 first email, day 4, day 8, day 14, then a final follow-up at day 21 or 30. 3 to 5 follow-ups at most. Subject lines like Did you see this? and Quick idea for {{company}} and the advice to skip Just following up.

## Weaknesses

- Last try on my end is a guilt-leaning subject.
- Plain-text templates are short and lightly personalized.
- Agency promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- Opening lines that sound human (just wanted to pop this back up).
- The adding-value test: why is this worth their time?
- Note a real launch or news item with a one-line idea.
