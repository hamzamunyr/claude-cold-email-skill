# GMass Follow-Up Breakdown

**Source:** GMass blog "How to Write a Cold Email Follow Up + 5 Templates" (2026), from a Gmail outreach tool vendor
**Source number:** 075 (follow-up source, 5 emails counted)
**Performance data:** Quotes studies (personalized subjects up 30.5%, 36 to 50 character subjects up 24.6%, 55% of replies from the 4th to 8th follow-up). Unverified.

> Note on the source: this is a follow-up guide. Only the subject lines are quoted; everything else is paraphrase.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trigger event | "Congratulations on [X], some ideas about achieving [goal]" | Congratulate a funding round, then ask whether they considered the proposal and offer a Wednesday call |
| 2 | After a meeting | "I'd love to hear your feedback on the meeting" | Ask what they thought |
| 3 | After a previous follow-up | "Re: [original subject]" | A short note in the thread |
| 4 | After tweaking the email | "Can you help me with this?" | A rewritten ask, a different angle |
| 5 | After several follow-ups | "Still any interest in our service?" | A last check on interest |

## Cadence and timing

First follow-up 2 days later, second 4 days after, third 7 days after that, fourth 14 days after that, fifth 30 days after. Change the subject when you follow up in the same thread. Do not begin with following up. Segment openers and non-openers.

## Weaknesses

- Template 1 assumes they remember a proposal a cold contact never saw.
- The can you help me subject is a manipulative curiosity trick.
- Segmenting by opens relies on unreliable open data.
- Tool promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- Subject variation inside the same thread.
- Do not start the body with following up; give the reason.
- The five-step spacing out to 30 days.
