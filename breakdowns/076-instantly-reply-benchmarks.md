# Instantly Reply Rate Benchmarks Breakdown

**Source:** Instantly blog "B2B Cold Email Response & Success Rates: Instantly's Benchmarks" (updated July 2026), from a cold email platform vendor
**Type:** practice and benchmark guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page gives ranges and cites outside studies (a large link-outreach study and a 16.5 million email benchmark). I could not verify any of them, so treat all figures as unverified vendor claims.

## Benchmarks it states

- Reply rate is replies divided by delivered emails (hard bounces excluded).
- It calls 5 to 10% solid for B2B, 10 to 15% excellent, and 15% or more best in class on tight segments. Broad lists on new domains are expected to land in low single digits.
- Published ranges conflict because "cold" sometimes includes warm intros, list quality differs, and many replies arrive after the first touch.
- Count a positive reply plus qualified neutral replies as the effective rate, and classify replies (positive, referral, objection, not now, unsubscribe, out of office).

## Practices extracted

- Fix deliverability before testing copy: authentication, complaints far below 0.3%, one-click unsubscribe, bounces under 2%.
- Keep emails about 50 to 125 words, one ask, a narrow next step (for example a short call with two time windows).
- Personalize to the problem and use event triggers (hiring bursts, tech changes, funding, new markets).
- Plan 1 to 3 follow-ups over 7 to 14 days, each with one new angle or proof point. The first follow-up is said to add a large share of replies.
- Test one variable at a time (subject, opener, proof, CTA, send window), cull losing variants quickly, and define success up front.
- Track positive reply rate, meetings, cost per meeting and time to first meeting, not only replies.
- Cap daily sends per inbox (it says 30 at most for new inboxes) and ramp slowly.

## Weaknesses

- Benchmarks are vendor-compiled and mix very different sending setups.
- Heavy product promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The reply rate formula and reply taxonomy, so results are comparable.
- The advice to judge by positive replies and meetings, not raw replies.
