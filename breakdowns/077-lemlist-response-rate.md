# lemlist Cold Email Response Rate Breakdown

**Source:** lemlist blog "Cold Email Response Rates in 2026" (August 2026, updated September 2026), from an outreach platform vendor
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page cites other vendors' analyses (an average reply rate of about 3.43% from over 20 million emails, a 16.5 million email study, list-size and personalization comparisons). I could not verify any of them, so treat every figure as an unverified vendor claim. The page itself notes every benchmark is a population average.

## Benchmarks it states

- Reply rate = replies divided by delivered emails (exclude bounces). Any response counts, including a decline or an out-of-office.
- Average reply rate about 3.43%; standard campaigns 1 to 5%; good 5 to 10%; top performers above 10%. A different widely quoted average of about 8.5% comes from a different dataset, which is why published numbers conflict.
- Smaller lists reply more: under 50 recipients about 5.8% versus about 2.1% above 1,000 (reported).
- Advanced, context-specific personalization is said to lift replies from about 7 to 9% to 17 to 18% (reported).
- About 42% of replies are said to come from follow-ups, and 48% of reps never send one.
- Seniority and size: C-suite and enterprise reply least, directors, managers and small companies reply more. Compare against your own industry before judging a rate.
- Open rates are unreliable (mail privacy features), so judge by replies.

## Practices extracted

- Fix deliverability before copy: authentication, warm-up, steady volume, complaints under 0.1%, bounces under 2%.
- Narrow the list to the ideal customer profile and verify every contact.
- Personalize with real context (funding, job change, tech stack, hiring), not just a first name.
- Subject lines short and specific (36 to 50 characters tested well, varies by audience); do not waste preview text on a greeting.
- One question or one next step, not both. Keep the body short.
- Add other channels to the sequence instead of only more emails.
- Contact people at high-intent moments (funding, job changes, hiring, site visits) and refresh lists, since static lists decay.
- Send by the recipient's time zone; the best day and time are a hypothesis to test, not a rule.
- A/B test one variable at a time.

## Weaknesses

- Figures are secondhand and unverified, and the page is a vendor pitch.
- "18%" is presented as attainable but depends on list size and targeting, not copy alone.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The systems view: list, deliverability and follow-ups matter more than clever copy.
- Smaller, verified lists beat big ones.
