# Woodpecker Cold Email Statistics Breakdown

**Source:** Woodpecker blog "Cold Email Statistics Based on Sending Over 20M Cold Emails" (2026 benchmarks), from a cold email platform vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Woodpecker template source (#004).
**Type:** statistics and practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page quotes its own platform data and outside studies (a 16.5 million email study, a platform benchmark report). I could not verify any figure, and several are re-quoted from other vendors, so treat everything as an unverified vendor claim.

## Figures it states (unverified)

- Average reply rate about 3.43%, down from about 5.1% in 2024; 5 to 10% good; above 10% excellent. Bounce rate averages about 5.1%, under 2% is good.
- Advanced personalization (custom lines about an article, a hire, a funding round, a specific pain) about 17 to 18% versus about 7 to 9% for basic.
- Smaller lists reply more: under 50 about 5.8%, 50 to 200 about 4 to 5%, 200 to 500 about 3%, 1,000 or more about 2.1%. Verified lists about twice the reply rate of unverified.
- Follow-ups: about 42% of replies come from follow-ups (58% from the first email), 48% of reps never follow up, one follow-up lifts total replies about 66%, the first follow-up is the strongest single step, and from the fourth follow-up the risk of complaints and unsubscribes rises.
- Open rates are inflated by mail privacy features, so use reply rate.

## Practices extracted

- Two campaigns example: 100 well-researched prospects can yield as many replies as 1,000 generic ones, with far less spam risk.
- Build small segments that share one trait (industry, size, tech stack, hiring signal, trigger) and one shared pain point.
- Use about 3 genuinely specific custom lines per email from public information only; do not guess private facts.
- Prospects look you up before replying, so keep LinkedIn and your site current and have proof ready.
- Many negative replies: check follow-up spacing first (too frequent or too regular reads as pressure), then strip salesy copy; throw out widely circulated templates.
- Keep emails under about 80 words (its claim); a 4 to 7 step sequence; vary the follow-up timing (about 3 to 4 days, then 5 to 7, then 7 to 10).
- Avoid subject lines that read as automation, such as "Quick question for you" and "Thought this might be useful"; specific, contextual subjects do better.
- A high open rate with a misleading subject poisons domain reputation.
- Elite teams test about 10 variations of the first email, then build the sequence around the winner.

## Weaknesses

- Mixes its own data with quoted outside figures, with unclear definitions.
- Reply splits conflict with other sources here that credit follow-ups with more replies.
- Vendor pitch.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The small-list math and the shared-trait segmenting advice.
- The warning that overused "quick question" style subjects now look automated.
- Check follow-up spacing before blaming the copy when replies turn negative.
