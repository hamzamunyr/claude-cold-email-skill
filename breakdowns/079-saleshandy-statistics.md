# Saleshandy Cold Email Statistics Breakdown

**Source:** Saleshandy blog "I Analyzed 30.31 Million Cold Emails: 13 Stats That Matter in 2026" (October 2026), from a cold email software vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Saleshandy sources (#055 and the templates source).
**Type:** statistics and practice guide (no full sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page reports results from its own platform dataset of about 30 million cold emails. I could not verify any of it, and I read the page through an extraction summary rather than the raw text, so treat every figure as an unverified vendor claim.

## Figures it states (unverified)

- Average reply rate about 4.5%; top 1% of campaigns 15 to 30%; top 5% 11 to 15%; average campaigns 3 to 10%; positive reply rate about 3 to 5%; average open rate about 21%.
- Smaller campaigns reply more: under 200 prospects about 15 to 20%, 200 to 500 about 11 to 13%, 500 to 1,000 about 8%.
- One clear soft call to action is said to earn about 78% more positive replies than a hard one.
- Follow-ups are said to bring about 57% of positive replies. First follow-up reply rate about 0.80% (29% of replies), follow-up 2 about 0.57% (15%), follow-up 3 about 0.56% (7%), follow-up 4 about 0.46% (3%), follow-up 5 or more about 0.37% (2%).
- Bounce rate by verification: valid about 1.2%, unverified about 2.1%, risky about 2.2%, bad about 10.6%.
- Top campaigns are said to book 2 to 3 meetings per 100 emails sent.
- Positive reply rate by channel mix: email only about 0.11%, email plus LinkedIn about 0.21%, email plus calls about 0.28%, three channels about 0.88%.
- An internal test of AI agents running outreach reported about 4% reply and 2% positive reply.

## Practices extracted

- Micro-segment before you write; open with context about them (a post, a hire) and lead with their problem.
- First-touch emails under 100 words; subject lines 5 to 7 words (36 to 50 characters), no colons or quotes, no all caps, and no fake Re: or Fwd:. Use their own words from public posts and reviews.
- Prefer a soft ask (for example "worth a quick chat?" or "does this resonate?") to a hard ask like a calendar link.
- Follow-ups introduce a new insight, use case or angle, not a repeat. Spacing by persona: decision makers every 2 to 3 days, users and managers every 24 to 48 hours (this source's own advice, shorter than most other sources).
- Verify the list before launch; monitor inbox placement per mailbox provider; warm up in the same industry you will prospect.
- Send Tuesday to Thursday, around 9 to 10 in the recipient's local time, and avoid late Friday.
- Match the target job title to company size. Add at least one other channel (LinkedIn or calls).
- Use AI for research and enrichment first, then sequences and replies.

## Weaknesses

- Vendor-only data, no outside check; the summary I read did not show the methodology.
- Its follow-up cadence and the reply share by step conflict with other sources here.
- Soft CTA examples in the summary are vague networking lines, which I do not recommend.
- Product promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The small-campaign gap, the verification-versus-bounce table and the soft-versus-hard ask finding.
- A subject line example that swaps a vague "quick question" for a specific one.
