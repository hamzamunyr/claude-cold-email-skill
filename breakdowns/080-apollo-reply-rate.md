# Apollo Reply Rate Benchmarks Breakdown

**Source:** Apollo Insights "Cold Email Reply Rates: What to Expect From a Well-Run Campaign" (May 2026), from a sales intelligence platform vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Apollo template source (#002).
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page re-quotes figures from other vendor blogs (an average around 3.1%, a 3 to 5.1% range, top performers at 8 to 12% or 15 to 25%). I could not verify any of them, so treat them as unverified claims.

## Benchmarks it states (unverified)

| Tier | Reply rate | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Below average | under 2% | Deliverability, targeting or generic messaging |
| Average, well-run | 3 to 5% | Solid targeting, reasonable personalization |
| Strong | 5 to 8% | Precise targeting, relevant messaging, good follow-up |
| Excellent | 10% or more | Signal-triggered outreach, tight niche |

For sales teams it suggests tracking positive reply rate: under 2 to 3% is a flag, 4 to 6% is a target, 8% or more is a stretch.

## Practices extracted

- Four sources of variance: list size, message relevance, deliverability, timing and follow-up. Smaller lists (50 or fewer) beat 1,000 or more.
- Deliverability gates everything: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, complaints under 0.3% (target 0.1%), warmed domains, verified contacts, Google Postmaster Tools.
- Lead with a trigger (funding, job change, product launch, tech adoption) rather than a persona template.
- Emails under about 150 words, one clear ask (a short call or a yes or no question), and a relevant stat or insight as the hook.
- Follow up: most replies arrive after the second or third touch.
- Diagnose in order: deliverability, then list quality, then messaging.
- Report positive reply rate, not total replies (which include out-of-office, unsubscribes and negatives).

## Weaknesses

- Secondhand benchmarks with no methodology.
- Heavy product promotion between sections.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The tiered table and the positive-reply targets.
- The diagnosis order when results are weak.
