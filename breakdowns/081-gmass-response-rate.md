# GMass Average Cold Email Response Rate Breakdown

**Source:** GMass blog "What's the Average Cold Email Response Rate in 2026?" (first published 2021, updated January 2026), from a Gmail mail merge tool vendor. This is a different page from the earlier GMass follow-up source (#075).
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page says the widely quoted average is 1 to 5%, that its own campaigns ranged from under 1% to over 25%, and re-quotes outside figures (personalized emails getting more than twice the replies, targeted emails at 15 to 25%, emails of 50 to 125 words getting reply rates "around 50%"). I could not verify any of them. The last figure is ambiguous: a reader comment on the page asks whether it means a 50% reply rate or 50% of other emails, and the page does not answer, so I do not use it.

## What it says

- Response rate is replies divided by emails sent, and open rate should be measured as unique opens. A big gap between a high open rate and a low reply rate suggests the message is not engaging.
- Four things move the rate: personalization, industry and offer, the call to action, and how warm the lead is. A generic benchmark for all industries is misleading.
- If the call to action is a link click or sign-up rather than a conversation, you are not measuring replies at all.
- Warm leads reply more, so remind them why they are warm.

## Practices extracted

- Personalize in a way that rings true: link to a real article, not a category page; fake personalization hurts.
- A/B test variations of subject lines and other elements on parts of your list, then send the winner to the rest.
- Filter and verify the list regularly; dead addresses hard bounce and raise spam suspicion; suppress unsubscribes and bounces automatically; do not keep sending follow-ups to people with no interest.
- Segment by behavior (for example, those who opened but did not reply) and use new messaging for them.
- Always include a clear call to action so the reader knows a reply is wanted.
- Keep it short and simple, roughly 50 to 125 words.

## Weaknesses

- Old article refreshed with secondhand figures; the "around 50%" claim is unclear.
- Advice to put the recipient's name in the subject line is basic personalization that other sources say no longer stands out.
- Tool promotion throughout.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The reminder to match the metric to the goal (a reply rate means little if the ask is a link click).
- The check that a large open to reply gap points to weak messaging.
- Link to a real, specific page when you cite their work.
