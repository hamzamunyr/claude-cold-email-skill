# Artisan B2B Response Rates Breakdown

**Source:** Artisan blog "Low B2B cold email response rates? Here's how to fix it", from an AI sales tool vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Artisan template and follow-up sources.
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide with 1 sample follow-up email (1 email added to the count)
**Performance data:** all figures are re-quoted from other studies (a 16.5 million email study, an email marketing study, a decision-maker survey, a sales tool's own campaign data). I read the page through an extraction summary, could not verify any figure, and treat them as unverified claims.

## Figures it states (unverified)

- Average B2B cold email response rate about 5.8%; one follow-up raises it by nearly 50%.
- Best send day Thursday (about 6.87% reply rate); late evening opens peak; but 69% of decision makers in one survey say time of year does not affect whether they reply.
- Emails with no question are said to get an 11% higher reply rate and a 25% higher interest rate than emails with questions (one tool's own data). One link is said to lift positive responses by 11% (same source).

## The sample email

A short follow-up: it refers to the last email about a possible partnership, restates a past client result, asks whether they want to learn more, and adds a P.S. asking whether they are the right person or can point to the right one. The opening "I'm following up on my last email" is the weak style other sources warn against; the P.S. right-person ask is the useful part.

## Practices extracted

- Measure response rate per sequence and per step, excluding bounces.
- Subject lines up to about 60 characters; state a clear use or evoke curiosity, not both. Its examples are generic ("quick question", "[company] & [your company]", "Partnership inquiry for [company]"), which other sources say read as automation.
- Personalize specifically and currently (a recent post, an award, a funding round); the page's examples lean on flattery ("blew me away", "phenomenal"), which I do not recommend.
- Introduction, an offer tied to their pain, and one line of proof.
- Three follow-ups, 3 to 5 days apart, referencing the first email, restating the offer, adding a case study or testimonial, and asking them to forward it to the right person.
- Use weekday daytime as a baseline and test; adjust for location; avoid weekends.
- Vet list data providers, verify from multiple sources, avoid scraped data, and warm up inboxes in small batches.
- Test a question-free, action-based call to action with at most one link to a booking page.

## Conflicts with other sources

- Ending on a question (most sources here) vs no question (this source, from one tool's data). Test both.
- No link in a first email (most sources) vs one link lifting positive replies (this source). Keep to at most one link and test.

## Weaknesses

- Re-quoted, unverifiable data and a vendor pitch.
- Flattering personalization examples and generic subjects.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The right-person P.S. on a follow-up.
- Asking for a forward to the decision maker in the later follow-ups.
- Measuring per step and per sequence.
