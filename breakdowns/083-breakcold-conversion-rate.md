# Breakcold Cold Email Conversion Rate Breakdown

**Source:** Breakcold blog "What Is A Bad, Good & Average Cold Email Conversion Rate? [2026 Guide]", from a sales CRM vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Breakcold template source (#018).
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** the page's "bad, average, good" conversion rates are built from assumed stage rates (open, reply, booking, closing), not measured data. I could not verify them and treat them as the author's illustration only.

## The model

Conversion rate (customers per emails sent) is the product of four stages: open, reply, booking and closing.

| Scenario (assumed) | Open | Reply | Booking | Closing | Result per 100 emails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Good | 70% | 15% | 50% | 80% | about 4.2 customers |
| Average | 55% | 6% | 35% | 60% | about 0.7 customers |
| Bad | 20% | 2% | 20% | 40% | about 0.03 customers |

**An arithmetic slip:** the page says the bad case means one customer per 320 emails, but 0.032 customers per 100 emails is about one per 3,000. The good and average cases check out (about 1 in 24 and 1 in 144). The assumed stage rates themselves are optimistic (a 70% open rate and 80% closing rate are rarely reported) and mix unlike things, so use the formula and substitute your own numbers.

## Practices extracted

- Judge a campaign end to end (open, reply, booking, close), not just replies; weak deliverability drags every stage down.
- Separate positive from negative replies, since only positive ones turn into bookings.
- Make the ask straight to the point to lift the booking rate.
- Either hyper-personalize (news, a podcast, a post they wrote, used for the first line and a tailored closing line) or segment your persona into narrow groups (for example one niche city and category) so a generic message still feels relevant.
- Send smaller batches of about 30 to 80 leads per campaign rather than one blast of 1,000.
- Warm leads on LinkedIn before the email to raise positive replies (its own claim).
- Compares email to cold calling (5 to 10% claimed) and LinkedIn selling (1.4 to 8.2% claimed). These comparisons are unsourced.

## Weaknesses

- Assumed numbers presented as benchmarks; one arithmetic error.
- Cold call and LinkedIn comparisons are unsupported.
- Heavy product promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The multiply-the-stages view of conversion.
- Small batches with a narrow segment instead of one large blast.
