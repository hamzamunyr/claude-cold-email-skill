# Martal B2B Cold Email Statistics Breakdown

**Source:** Martal blog "B2B Cold Email Statistics 2026: Benchmarks & What Works Now" (April 2026, updated August 2026), from an outbound agency. This is a different page from the earlier Martal sources (#058, #064).
**Type:** statistics roundup (no sample emails, no email count added). I read it through an extraction summary.
**Performance data:** the page is a collection of figures quoted from other vendors (a platform benchmark, a deliverability vendor, a CRM vendor, a link-outreach study and others). I could not verify any of them, and some conflict with each other, so treat all as unverified.

## Figures it quotes (unverified)

- Average reply rate about 3.43% (down from about 5.1%); open rate about 27.7%; conversion roughly 0.2 to 2%; bounce about 7 to 8% on average and under 2% for the best.
- Personalization beyond a first name lifts replies (up to about 18% vs about 9%), yet only about 5% of senders personalize every email.
- About 42% of replies come from follow-ups, 48% of reps never send one.
- Contradictory contact-count claims: emailing several contacts at one company is quoted as raising response by 93% in one place, while other pages here advise capping contacts per company.
- Subject line stats (numbers, questions, 36 to 50 characters) and send-time stats (launch Monday, follow up Wednesday, 9:30 to 11:30 local time, avoid Friday) are all quoted secondhand.
- 50 to 125 words is quoted as the best reply length; email plus LinkedIn and phone is quoted as lifting results.

## Practices extracted

- Authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), complaints under 0.1%, one-click unsubscribe, a dedicated sending domain or subdomain, gradual warm-up.
- Clean and verify the list; keep bounces under 2%.
- Multi-point personalization (company, role, recent trigger, peer proof) and custom opening lines.
- Low-commitment ask (a 10 to 15 minute conversation); A/B test subject, CTA wording and length.
- Include an easy opt-out and keep unsubscribes at or under about 2%.

## Weaknesses

- A pile of re-quoted numbers with little context, some contradictory.
- Lists "I hope you are doing well" as a feature of successful emails, which other sources say to avoid.
- Agency promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- A single list of the main deliverability and list-quality rules in one place.
- A reminder that the benchmarks disagree, so benchmark against yourself.
