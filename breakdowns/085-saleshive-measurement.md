# SalesHive Cold Email Measurement Breakdown

**Source:** SalesHive blog "Cold Email Response Rate: Measurement and Testing Guide" (originally 2018, updated October 2026), from an outsourced sales agency
**Type:** measurement and testing guide (no sample emails, no email count added). I read it through an extraction summary.
**Performance data:** it quotes sector and timing figures without clear sources. I could not verify them, so treat them as unverified.

## Practices extracted (the useful part is the method)

- **Calculate reply rate from delivered emails**, not sent, and separate total replies from positive replies. Track meetings booked and bounce rate beside them.
- **Pick one unit** (per delivered email for campaign health, per contact for audience performance) and use it everywhere. Ignore open rate as a KPI.
- **Diagnose before rewriting copy:** if reply rate suddenly drops, check inbox placement and bounce rate first; pause and clean the list if bounces are high (under 2%).
- **Valid A/B tests have three parts:** a clear hypothesis, one changed variable, and enough data. Its floor is about 250 contacts per variant, ideally 500 or more, a 95% confidence rule, and a run of two to six weeks.
- **Match the metric to what you tested:** open rate for subject lines, reply and positive reply for the body or offer, meeting-booked rate for call to action tests.
- **Reporting rhythm:** review weekly to catch deliverability dips, decide A/B winners monthly.
- **Targeting:** narrow your ideal customer profile (one client reportedly moved from 2% to 11% by narrowing), reach one or two contacts per company (quoted 7.8% vs 3.8% for 10 or more), and prefer smaller campaigns (under 100 recipients).
- **Timing hooks:** a timeline-based ask (for example, worth 15 minutes this week) is quoted as booking meetings at over three times the rate of a problem-based ask.
- Turning off open-tracking pixels was reported to lift replies in an agency experiment.
- Cap mailbox volume at 30 to 40 a day, warm domains for 4 weeks, use verified multi-source data, authenticate with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, keep emails under about 80 words with one binary question or simple request, 1 to 3 follow-ups over 7 to 14 days.

## Weaknesses

- Numbers without named sources.
- It gives a warm-up rule without discussing the fake-engagement debate; see the practices guide.
- Agency promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The testing standard (hypothesis, one variable, sample size, confidence, duration, matching metric).
- The diagnose-first order and the reply-from-delivered formula.
