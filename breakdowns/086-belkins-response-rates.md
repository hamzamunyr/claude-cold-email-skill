# Belkins Cold Email Response Rates Breakdown

**Source:** Belkins "What are B2B Cold Email Response Rates? (2026 Study)", from an outbound agency. It analyzes its own client campaigns. I read it through an extraction summary.
**Type:** statistics study (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** this is first-party data from about 7.5 million cold B2B emails sent in 2025 (about 34,000 replies, over 1,200 booked appointments). I could not verify it independently, but it is the original source for figures other vendors quote, so it matters.

## Important finding: the original numbers are much lower than the quotes

The page reports an **average reply rate of about 0.45%** across all 2025 campaigns, not the 3 to 6% figures that many vendor pages attribute to Belkins. The study measures client campaigns that are largely large-scale B2B outreach, so its mix differs from a small, tightly targeted campaign. That does not prove the other numbers wrong, but it shows the benchmarks you see online are not comparable. Treat quoted "Belkins" figures on other pages with caution.

## What the study reports

- Reply rate by month: highest about 0.54% (February), lowest about 0.35% (December); first half of 2025 about 0.50%, second half about 0.40%.
- By seniority: founders and owners about 0.57%, C-level about 0.42%, VPs about 0.32%. Founders reply about 35% more than C-level and about 80% more than VPs.
- By company size: 0 to 10 employees about 0.72%, 11 to 50 about 0.49%, 10,000 or more about 0.22%.
- By time: morning sends (8 to 12) about 0.54%, early morning about 0.52%, late evening about 0.40%. Wednesday and Thursday about 0.48%; an unusually high Sunday rate (about 0.57%) came from a very small volume.
- By industry and country: food and beverage the highest (about 3.47%), several service industries about 0.56 to 0.60%, banking and insurance lower; Poland about 1.43%, Canada about 0.63%, US about 0.51%, UK about 0.48%.

## Practices it recommends

- Target the right person, not the highest title: directors, budget influencers and founders of smaller companies.
- Match your approach to company size: for enterprise use multi-threaded outreach across departments, account-based personalization and longer nurture.
- Send Wednesday or Thursday mornings (8 to noon); avoid Friday afternoons and late evenings.
- Put your highest-effort campaigns into industries that respond, and reduce volume (not effort) in low-response ones.
- Quality over quantity: fewer, better-targeted emails.

## Weaknesses

- Results come from one agency's campaigns, so they may not apply to your market.
- Small-volume cells (Sunday, some countries) are noisy.

## What is genuinely useful here

- Evidence that large-scale cold outreach averages well under 1%, which resets expectations.
- The seniority and company-size patterns, and the "right person, not highest title" advice.
