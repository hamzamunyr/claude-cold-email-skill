# Saleshandy Improve Response Rate Breakdown

**Source:** Saleshandy blog "How to Improve Cold Email Response Rate? Tips + Templates" (2024, updated 2026), from a cold email software vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Saleshandy sources.
**Type:** practice guide with 4 sample emails (4 added to the count)
**Performance data:** I read the page through an extraction summary and could not verify the figures, so treat them as unverified vendor claims.

## The emails

| # | Template | Subject line | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem solution first email | "{{First name}}, I think I have found a solution to your problem!" | Where I found you, a recent achievement, a time-consuming task, two lines on the product, 15 minutes next week |
| 2 | First follow-up | "{{First Name}}, I am waiting for you" | You might be busy, did you read my last email, a 15 minute meeting this week |
| 3 | Second follow-up | "{{First name}}, any update?" | Assume they read it, offer a similar case study link |
| 4 | Sales email | "{{Prospect's Company}} + {{Your company}} = Great team." | Read about your goal in an article, how the product helps, a call this week |

## Practices extracted

- Deliverability basics (warm-up, authentication, daily quota), a company address rather than a freemail one, a short signature, a clear subject and preheader (40 to 130 characters), proofread.
- Short, mostly plain email with one call to action; send on weekdays; 4 to 5 gentle follow-ups. It also suggests emojis and an urgent call-to-action button, which other sources here advise against.
- Personalize with a real achievement or article and frame the product as a solution.

## Weaknesses

- the follow-up subjects (I am waiting for you, any update?) and a line assuming they must have had time to read the last email are guilt-flavored, the first email claims to have wondered about them for days, and the whole set leans on filler.
- Vendor promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The structure of the problem email: where you found them, a recent achievement, the area they may be struggling with, a two-line solution, a 15 minute ask.
