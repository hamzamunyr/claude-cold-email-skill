# Hunter State of Cold Email Breakdown

**Source:** Hunter "The State of Email Outreach 2026" report, from an email finder and outreach vendor. This is a different source from the earlier Hunter templates.
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** This is a first-party report built on about 31 million emails sent by its users in 2025 plus surveys of senders and recipients. It is the largest dataset in the repo that reports behavior differences, but it is the vendor's own, unaudited data. I read the page through an extraction summary and could not verify the figures, so treat them as unverified vendor claims.

## Figures it states (unverified)

- Average sequence reply rate about 4.5%, open rate about 30%, bounce about 3.6%, unsubscribe about 1.6%; about 1 in 20 cold emails gets a reply. By type: sales sequences about 3%, marketing about 6.2%, headhunting about 7.5%, digital PR about 13%.
- Without open tracking: about 7.4% replies vs about 4.4% with it (+68%). Custom domain about 5.2% vs freemail about 2.5%. Sequences of 21 to 50 recipients about 6.2% vs 500 or more about 2.4%. 20 to 49 sends per account per day about 5.7% vs 4.5% average. One or two people per company about 5.1% vs 3.5% for three or more. Three messages vs one: about 6.8% vs 3.3% (+106%).
- Two custom attributes in the email lift replies about 56% over none (5.6% vs 3.6%); manually edited emails beat fully automated ones by about 18% (5.2% vs 4.4%); Google Workspace senders reply more (about 5.9%) than Microsoft 365 (about 4%).
- Survey of decision makers: about 69% are bothered if AI was used unless it feels human; about 65% say cold email fails by feeling too sales-focused; about 61% cite irrelevance; about 67% reply more when personalization uses public information; about 48% call out generic messages; LinkedIn is the preferred channel for about 50% vs email about 25%.

## Practices extracted

- Do not track opens or clicks unless you need to; send from a business domain, not freemail.
- Keep sequences to 50 recipients or fewer, 20 to 49 sends per account per day, one or two contacts per company.
- Follow up but stop at about three total messages; personalize with two custom attributes; edit manually when it matters; make AI-assisted copy feel human.
- Use low-pressure asks (can I send more info? open to learning more?), subject lines of 5 to 6 words; validate lists with several enrichment sources and filter catch-alls.

## Weaknesses

- Re-quoted or self-reported benchmarks with no clear method, and some conflict with other sources here.
- Vendor promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The largest set of behavior comparisons in the repo: no open tracking, business domains, small sequences, one or two contacts per company, three messages, manual editing, and the survey on AI and personalization.
