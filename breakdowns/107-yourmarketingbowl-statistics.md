# Your Marketing Bowl Statistics Breakdown

**Source:** Your Marketing Bowl blog "Cold email benchmarks 2026: reply rate, open rate & timing", from a marketing agency
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no full sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** A roundup of figures attributed to five or more studies, with sub-claims that conflict. I could not verify the figures, so treat them as unverified vendor claims. I read the page through an extraction summary.

## Figures and claims

- Average reply about 3.43% (top performers 10% or more); one study at 5.8% and another at 8.2% for the same year show how much the sources differ.
- Personalization lift quoted at 142% and a table of open and reply rates by personalization level (none about 5.6%, basic about 9%, advanced about 11.6%).
- Follow-ups: first follow-up peaks, fifth follow-up about 3.8%; about 42% of replies come from follow-ups and 48% of reps never send one.
- Bounce reasons quoted: about 49% invalid or outdated addresses, 23% domain reputation, 17% aggressive sending, 11% missing authentication. Inbox placement quoted near 84% globally.

## Practices extracted

- Disable open tracking pixels to potentially lift replies; keep daily sends under 100 per inbox; warm domains; set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
- Keep the first email 50 to 125 words (top performers under 80); send 2 to 3 follow-ups (avoid going past 4); mid-week, 9:30 to 11:30 local time; avoid December and late weekends; add light LinkedIn nurturing.
- Its sample subject lines are six invented examples tied to made-up recipients, for example promising a cost-per-lead cut or booked demos, which would be fabricated claims if reused, so I do not adopt them.

## Weaknesses

- It is a stats roundup with conflicting numbers and fabricated-looking sample subjects.
- Vendor promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The bounce-reason breakdown (most bounces are bad data, not content).
