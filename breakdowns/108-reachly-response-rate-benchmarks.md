# Reachly Response Rate Benchmarks Breakdown

**Source:** Reachly blog "Cold Email Response Rates: 2026 Benchmarks" (about 920 million emails across five datasets), from an outreach tool vendor. This is a different page from the earlier Reachly template source.
**Type:** benchmark and practice guide (no full sample emails, no email count added)
**Performance data:** It compiles other vendors' datasets and does not publish its own. I could not verify the figures, so treat them as unverified vendor claims. I read the page through an extraction summary.

## Figures and claims

- The published average is about 3.4 to 3.7% per email delivered, but one large dataset (about 850 million emails, replies per contact) shows the median sender reaches only about 0.74% of contacts, so half of senders sit below one reply per 135 people emailed. The top 10% clear about 10.7% and the top 5% of campaigns about 11 to 15% (other vendors' figures).
- Replies per contact differ from replies per email, which explains much of the conflict across sources.
- Follow-ups carry about 42% of replies and 44% of positive replies; one follow-up lifts total replies about 66%; 3 to 5 follow-up steps about 8.3% vs 4.1% for a single email; segments under 200 prospects about 15 to 20%; verified contacts bounce about 1.5% vs 2.6% unverified; 50 to 80 words reported best.

## Practices extracted

- Divide by delivered emails; set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC and verify warm-up; move cold sending off the primary domain; verify every address and cut unverified contacts.
- Tighten the ICP definition, add a buying signal, keep segments under 200, rewrite the first line around a real event, run 4 to 6 follow-ups that change the angle each time, add a LinkedIn touch, answer replies fast.

## Weaknesses

- Compilation of unverified figures; some practices (4 to 6 follow-ups) are longer than the skill default.
- Vendor promotion.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The distinction between replies per contact and per email, and the median-sender figure that resets expectations.
