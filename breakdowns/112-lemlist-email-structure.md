# lemlist Cold Email Structure Breakdown

**Source:** lemlist blog "The 7-Part Cold Email Structure That Got Us a 50% Reply Rate (Real Example)" (November 2025, updated September 2026), from an outreach tool vendor
**Template number:** 112 (one source, 1 email analyzed: a partnership email shown as an image, which I viewed)
**Subject line:** "Collaborate?" with a fist emoji (the page also says to swap integration for collaboration for content partnerships)
**Performance data:** the page says the email got a 90% open rate and a 50% reply rate, but on only 60 sends, and it admits the sample is too small to generalize. The audience was friendly SaaS founders. Open rates are unreliable, so I treat both numbers as an anecdote, not a benchmark. Other figures on the page (a 46% vs 35% open rate for personalized subjects from a large study, 2 to 4 word subjects opening best) are quoted secondhand and unverified.

## The email (paraphrase)

An informal partnership email: a greeting with the first name, a line saying they are a big fan of the company with a personalized detail merged in, a one-line introduction of the sender and what the company does, an ask to discuss a potential integration that claims strong market demand in its own data, a request to get on a call about a win-win partnership, and a casual are-you-in close, then a name signature.

## The 7-part structure the page teaches

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Subject line | Get the open: specific, no clickbait or all caps, short, test it |
| Greeting | Always the first name; fall back to the company team or reference an event |
| Intro line | One genuine, specific sentence (a post, launch or podcast), not a generic compliment |
| Story or body | Tie one specific pain point to the offer; about 120 words at most |
| Call to action | One clear, short, low-friction next step, proportionate to a stranger |
| Signature | First name, title with a company link, LinkedIn; no heavy HTML or extra links |
| P.S. (optional) | A case study or a second hook |

## Psychology used

Liking through a compliment, commitment minimization (a call, kept casual), mutual benefit (win-win partnership), specificity (a personalized line), and a claim of market demand (unsupported).

## Hook, CTA and personalization

- **Hook:** a compliment with a merged personalized detail.
- **CTA:** a call to discuss a partnership.
- **Personalization:** one merged detail per company; the page says the compliment must be genuine and meaningful.

## Weaknesses

- The email leans on big-fan flattery, an unsupported market-demand claim and a slangy close, and it asks for a partnership call before giving a reason.
- It uses a merge tag for the compliment, which generic data will make hollow, and an emoji in the subject.
- 60 sends to a friendly niche is an anecdote.

## What is genuinely useful here

- The seven-part checklist (subject, greeting, intro, story, one call to action, plain signature, optional P.S.).
- Common structure mistakes: a vague subject, burying the purpose, a wall of text over about 120 words, competing calls to action, an over-designed signature, a generic intro, and no clear next step.
- Make the ask proportional (15 minutes for a stranger, not 45).
