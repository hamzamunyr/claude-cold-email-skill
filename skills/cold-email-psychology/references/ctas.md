# Calls to Action

Emails analyzed so far: 191 (Clay, Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, Coursera, FirstSales, Smartlead, SmartWriter, Cognism, LeadsMonky). Woodpecker counts are approximate.

| CTA type | Example shape | When it works | Count | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open call or meeting ask | "Do you have time this week to talk?" | Warm leads, senior prospects, strong proof. Higher friction | 42 | Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker, Coursera, Smartlead, SmartWriter, Cognism |
| Interest check | "If I could help with [problem], would that be useful?" or "Could this work for [company]?" | Cold first touch. Best default | 24 | Clay, Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, FirstSales, Smartlead, SmartWriter, Cognism, LeadsMonky |
| Short call ask (time-boxed) | "Can we do a 10 minute call?" | Value is clear and the prospect is senior. Name the duration | 37 | Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, Coursera, FirstSales, Smartlead, SmartWriter, LeadsMonky |
| Register or click a link | "Use the link below to save your place" | Event or session invites, trials, sign-ups | 14 | Woodpecker, SmartWriter, Cognism |
| Short asset offer | "Can I send a short video, breakdown, case study or menu?" | You have something quick to show. Lowest friction | 13 | Clay, Apollo, Woodpecker, Cognism, LeadsMonky |
| Calendar link | A link to book a time | After interest is shown, or for recruiter office hours | 7 | Apollo, Woodpecker, SmartWriter |
| Reply with feedback or a pick | "Tell me which of these are a no, and why" | You sent a short list. Easy to answer and useful for you | 4 | Woodpecker |
| Coffee or LinkedIn connect | "Could I invite you for a coffee?" | Networking, not sales | 4 | Woodpecker |
| Specific time options | "Do you have time Tuesday or Wednesday?" | Warm leads or strong proof. Two named options beat "when are you free?" | 8 | Salesloft, FirstSales, Smartlead, SmartWriter, Cognism |
| Show-me offer | "Want me to walk you through it?" | You have a clear demo of the idea you just described | 3 | Clay, FirstSales |
| Open offer of help | "Let me know if there is anything else I can help with" | Resource shares and warm follow-ups. Low pressure, low reply rate | 5 | Salesloft, Smartlead |
| Referral or recommendation ask | "If you know someone who is buying or selling, please keep me in mind" | After a successful result, when the client is happy | 2 | Woodpecker |
| Opinion ask | "I'd love to know what you think of it" | You shared an article or insight. Easy to answer in one line | 1 | Salesloft |
| No-oriented question | Ask if they would be against hearing how peers do it | Skeptical or senior buyers. Keep the wording simple | 1 | Salesloft |
| Invite offer | "Want an invite to the session?" | Free event or session that educates first | 1 | Clay |
| Referral ask (wrong person) | "Is there someone else I should speak to?" | Final follow-up, or when the contact may be wrong | 5 | Clay, Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, FirstSales, Cognism |
| Phone number | Give a phone number to call if interested | Weak in cold email. Adds an extra step | 2 | Salesloft, Cognism |
| Direct meeting ask | "Do you have 15 minutes this week?" | Only when value is already obvious. Raises resistance in a cold first email | 2 (Clay advises against, Salesloft uses one) | Clay, Salesloft |
| No-ask close | A plain line saying you are not asking for anything | High-value accounts or resource shares. Builds goodwill, weak reply rate | 3 | ZoomInfo, Smartlead |
| Reply if I am wrong | Invite a reply if your assumption is off, and offer to set a time | Final follow-up. Assume it is not a priority and leave the door open | 3 | ZoomInfo, FirstSales |
| Two-answer question | Ask if it is on their list now, or if you should check back later | A short nudge. Both answers move things forward | 3 | FirstSales, Smartlead |
| Reply either way | Ask for a quick reply whether yes or no | A follow-up with a new angle. Lowers the bar to answer | 1 | FirstSales |
| Dialogue question | Ask a real question about how they handle it today, with no meeting ask | Founders and senior buyers. Builds a conversation first | 1 | Smartlead |
| What would need to be true | Ask what would need to be true for your solution to make sense now | A day 7 follow-up with a different angle | 1 | Smartlead |
| Introduction request | Ask someone to introduce you to a person they know, and offer a forwardable note | When a real mutual contact exists. Easier if you keep the ask small | 2 | SmartWriter |
| Free access for a review | Offer a free account in return for an honest review | Creators and reviewers. Be clear about what you are asking for | 1 | SmartWriter |
| Ready draft question | Ask if a ready draft fits their audience | You can send finished work, such as a guest post | 1 | SmartWriter |
| Small favor ask | Ask for a light favor such as subscribing to your podcast, in a break-up email | A final email with a warm tone. Use only if it fits your brand | 1 | Cognism |
| Announce a follow-up call | Say you will likely follow up by phone in a couple of days, with a number to call | Only if you really will, and the prospect expects calls. Can feel pushy | 1 | Cognism |

Rule: one CTA per email. Phrase it as a small offer, not a request for their time. About half of the Woodpecker templates ask for a call outright, and Apollo and Salesloft use short call asks in several templates, while Clay advises against asking for a call in a first email. Default to a smaller ask, and use a call ask for senior prospects when the proof is strong. Do not stack a link, an opinion ask and a phone number in one email. Avoid assumptive or guilt-based closings (for example, assuming they surely have time).
