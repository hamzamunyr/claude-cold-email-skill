# Calls to Action

Emails analyzed so far: 12 (Clay, Apollo).

| CTA type | Example shape | When it works | Count | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short asset offer | "Can I send a short video, breakdown or case study showing how?" | You have something quick to show. Lowest friction | 4 | Clay, Apollo |
| Interest check | "If I could help with [problem], would that be useful?" or "Could this work for [company]?" | Cold first touch. Best default | 4 | Clay, Apollo |
| Short call ask (time-boxed) | "Can we do a 10 minute call?" | Value is clear and the prospect is senior. Name the duration | 3 | Apollo |
| Show-me offer | "Want me to walk you through it?" | You have a clear demo of the idea you just described | 2 | Clay |
| Invite offer | "Want an invite to the session?" | Free event or session that educates first | 1 | Clay |
| Referral ask (breakup) | "Is there someone else I should speak to?" | Final follow-up, or when the contact may be wrong | 1 | Clay |
| Calendar link | A link plus two specific time blocks | Only after the prospect shows interest | 1 (conditional) | Apollo |
| Direct meeting ask | "Do you have 15 minutes this week?" | Only when value is already obvious. Raises resistance in a cold first email | 1 (advised against) | Clay |

Rule: one CTA per email. Phrase it as a small offer, not a request for their time. Clay advises against asking for a call in the first email, while Apollo uses short call asks in half its templates. Default to a smaller ask, and use a call ask for senior prospects when the proof is strong.
