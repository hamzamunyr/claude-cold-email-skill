# Example Emails

All examples are original. Bracketed items are placeholders to fill with real facts. Never invent proof.

---

## Example 1: Returns software for an ecommerce brand

**Prospect:** Head of Customer Experience at a Shopify fashion brand
**Psychology:** specificity (job post trigger), loss aversion, social proof, commitment minimization
**Pattern from:** Clay (#001), five-part framework

**Subject options**
1. returns at [Brand]
2. your support hire
3. quick question on returns

**Email**

Hi [First name],

Your open support role lists processing returns by hand as a main duty, so I'm guessing returns eat a big chunk of your team's week.

We automate return requests, labels and refund checks, so agents only handle the odd cases.

When peak season hits, how will you keep returns from pulling agents off live chat?

[Similar brand] [real result] in [timeframe] after switching.

Would a 2 minute walkthrough be useful?

[Your name]

**Why it works:** the first line proves research, the question shows a cost they may not have priced in, the proof is a similar company, and the ask is tiny.

## Example 2: Local marketing agency for a dental clinic

**Prospect:** Owner of a small dental clinic
**Psychology:** specificity (website visit), reciprocity (free ideas), curiosity gap, effort reduction
**Pattern from:** Clay (#001), idea-list framework

**Subject options**
1. ideas for [Clinic]
2. three patient ideas
3. saw your website

**Email**

Hi [First name],

I was looking through [Clinic]'s website and had a few ideas for bringing in new patients. Have you tried any of these?

- Offer nearby gym members a first-visit checkup
- Give current patients a "bring a friend" bonus
- Add a page for [service you saw on their site] aimed at people searching nearby

We set up and run all three for local practices, so your team stays focused on patients.

Want me to send a short plan for [Clinic]?

[Your name]

**Why it works:** the ideas show real homework, the plan offer is a small ask, and the prospect gets value even if they never reply.

Congrats on [funding round]. With [number] engineering roles open on your careers page, I'd guess hiring fast is a priority right now.

We recently filled [number] engineering roles for [similar-stage company] in [timeframe], and I wrote up how the search ran.

[Your name]

**Why it works:** the event proves attention, the peer result is relevant to their stage, and the ask is a short read, not a meeting.

## Example 4: Accounting firm for a construction company owner

**Prospect:** Owner of a mid-size construction company
**Psychology:** normalization (shared problem), reciprocity (useful resource), no-oriented question, effort reduction
**Pattern from:** Salesloft (#003), shared-problem icebreaker with a resource

**Subject options**
1. job costing at [Company]
2. a one-page checklist
3. [First name], quick note

**Email**

Hi [First name],

Most construction owners I talk with say tracking job costs eats more of their week than it should.

I put together a one-page checklist on how similar firms catch cost overruns early: [link]. No signup needed.

Would you be against me sending a version tailored to [Company]?

[Your name]

**Why it works:** naming a common problem makes it easy to admit, the checklist gives value first, and a question where "no" is the safe answer keeps pressure low.

## Example 5: Commercial property broker for a growing company

**Prospect:** Head of Operations at a company hiring several people in one city
**Psychology:** specificity (hiring signal), reciprocity (curated shortlist), commitment minimization (feedback ask), effort reduction
**Pattern from:** Woodpecker (#004), curated list with feedback ask

**Subject options**
1. three offices for [Company]
2. space for your [city] team
3. a short list, [First name]

**Email**

Hi [First name],

I saw [Company] is hiring [number] people in [city], so I pulled three office spaces that fit a team of about [number]:

- [Address 1], [one key detail]
- [Address 2], [one key detail]
- [Address 3], [one key detail]

If any are an instant no, tell me which and why, and I'll refine the list before you spend time on it.

Want details on any of them?

[Your name]

**Why it works:** the shortlist proves effort and relevance, the "tell me which is a no" ask is easy to answer, and the reply gives you what you need to improve the list.

## Example 6: Security training provider for a law firm IT lead

**Prospect:** IT or operations manager at a mid-size law firm
**Psychology:** no-ask framing, specificity (a real detail about their firm), authority (original findings), reciprocity
**Pattern from:** ZoomInfo (#005), no-ask outreach

**Subject options**
1. impressed by [Firm]'s client portal
2. a note on [Firm]'s portal rollout
3. [First name], no ask

**Email**

Hi [First name],

I read about [Firm]'s new client portal and liked [specific detail you actually noticed].

Firms that open a portal to clients often run into [specific, real risk]. We work with firms like [real client, if permitted] on this, and I put together a one-page summary of what we see most often.

No ask. If it would help, reply "send it" and I'll forward it.

[Your name]

**Why it works:** the compliment is specific, the free summary gives value first, and saying "no ask" keeps pressure low. The tiny reply keeps a door open without a meeting request.
