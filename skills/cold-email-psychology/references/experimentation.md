# Campaign Experimentation

Load this only in Full mode. Purpose: test messaging and targeting without fooling yourself. Never invent performance data. Results are read with performance-analytics.md.

## 1. What can be tested

Audience segment, offer, messaging angle, subject line, opening line, call to action, follow-up timing, sequence structure, channel mix. Change one thing at a time.

## 2. Designing an experiment

1. **Hypothesis:** one sentence, falsifiable. "A hiring-signal opener will produce a higher qualified-meeting rate than a peer-result opener for ops managers at 50 to 200 person firms."
2. **One variable:** change only the element under test. Hold audience, offer, sending setup, timing and sequence length constant. If you must test two things, run separate tests or a deliberate factorial design with enough volume.
3. **Randomize:** split the same audience randomly into variants (for example by shuffling). Do not give the "best" contacts to your favorite variant.
4. **Pre-register:** before sending, write down the primary metric, the minimum meaningful difference, the sample size per variant, the run length and the stop rule.
5. **Sample size:** reply and meeting events are rare, so many sends are needed. As a rough guide from the sources, vendors suggest about 100 to 250 contacts per variant as a floor and 500 or more preferred, but that is only enough to see very large differences in reply rate and far too small for meeting or customer rates. Compute the number you need for your baseline rate and the lift you care about; if you cannot reach it, treat results as directional only.
6. **Run to a pre-set end:** two to six weeks including follow-ups and a wait of several days after the last touch before reading final reply rates. Do not stop at the first good-looking number.
7. **Keep a log:** every variant's exact copy, audience definition, send dates, volumes and notes.

## 3. Common mistakes

- Changing subject, offer and audience at once, then crediting the subject.
- Comparing this week's campaign with last month's.
- Declaring a winner after a handful of replies.
- Optimizing open rate and ending up with clickbait that hurts replies and reputation.
- Ignoring negative replies and complaints, which are costly signals.
- Adding follow-ups to rescue a weak list or offer.
