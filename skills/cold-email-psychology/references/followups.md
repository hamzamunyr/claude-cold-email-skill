# Follow-Up Playbook

Distilled from 15 follow-up guides (breakdowns #061 to #075: Warmup Inbox, Artisan, Smartlead x2, Martal, lemlist, Instantly, Sendr, Jacob Tuwiner, Ashley Gainer, Mixmax, Coldreach, Gong, SalesCaptain, GMass), plus the earlier cadence notes in frameworks.md. Most numbers are vendor or third-party claims and the sources disagree, so treat figures as starting points to test, not facts.

## The one rule

Every follow-up must carry something the last email did not: a new fact, a proof point, a sharper question, a different angle or an easier way to answer. If you cannot name what is new, do not send it. (Martal, Coldreach, Artisan, Smartlead, Sendr, Mixmax, lemlist agree.) A bump with no new substance is the most common mistake; one source reports such emails book far fewer meetings than follow-ups with substance, and that "Thoughts?" and "Never heard back" hurt meetings booked.

## How many follow-ups (sources conflict)

| Source view | Number | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Artisan, Smartlead, Warmup Inbox | 2 to 3 follow-ups | Returns shrink after that |
| Martal, Coldreach | 3 to 4 follow-ups (4 to 5 emails total) | Longer for big accounts, fewer for weak fit |
| Jacob Tuwiner | 4 follow-ups, then monthly | Self-reported results |
| lemlist | 4 to 9 follow-ups | Its own data, counts cumulative replies |
| Ashley Gainer (freelancers) | 2 to 3 follow-ups | Then stop |

Skill default: 3 follow-ups (4 emails), up to 4 for a high-value account with real research. Never add touches to fix a weak reply rate: fix targeting and the first email first.

## Timing

- First follow-up: 2 to 3 business days after the first email. Not the next day (reported to lower replies).
- Then widen the gaps: roughly 2, 4, 7, 14 days (Mixmax, GMass, Jacob) or 3, 4, 7 days (Smartlead, Martal).
- Scale gaps with seniority: small business every 3 to 5 days, mid-market 4 to 7, enterprise 7 to 10, C-suite 10 to 14 (Martal).
- Vary the time of day for each touch. Morning vs afternoon advice conflicts, so test it.
- Count business days, and avoid a Monday morning landing.
- Break-up: after the last touch, then a pause, then a re-engagement email months later.

## Reply in the thread or start a new one

- Default: reply in the same thread (context is free). The subject is then the original, with Re: appearing naturally because it is a real reply.
- Start a new thread with a new subject only when the angle truly changes (a new trigger or a different problem). (Martal, GMass)
- Never fake "Re:" on a first email. (see practices.md)

## What each touch is for

| Touch | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The full case | Why them, why now, one small ask |
| Follow-up 1 (day 2 to 3) | Add a new fact | A trigger, an observation or a small resource; one question |
| Follow-up 2 (day 6 to 10) | Add proof | A similar company, a number you can back up, or a case study offer |
| Follow-up 3 (day 12 to 18) | A different angle | A new problem framing, a pain hypothesis, or a direct qualifying question |
| Last | Close the loop | Short, no guilt, door open; or the wrong-person question |
| Later (60 to 90 days) | Re-engage | What changed since last time, tied to what they said |

## Follow-up types that earn their place

- **New fact first line.** Lead with the news, then one reason it matters to them, then a question answerable in a word. (Martal, Coldreach)
- **Pain hypothesis.** State your best guess and invite correction. (Coldreach)
- **Silence as multiple choice.** Name the likely reasons (priorities moved, an internal concern, timing) and ask which. (Martal)
- **Wrong-person check.** Cheapest ask: point me to whoever owns this. (Martal, Coldreach, Sendr)
- **Small win.** Give one idea they can use now with no strings. (Smartlead)
- **Proof point.** A similar company and a result you can back, admitting it may not map. (Instantly, Coldreach, Artisan)
- **Direct qualification question.** Are you working on [metric] or not a priority right now? (Coldreach)
- **Resource even if not now.** (Coldreach, Sendr, Warmup Inbox)
- **Trigger event.** The event is the whole reason for the email. (Martal, Sendr, GMass)
- **Re-engagement.** After months, say what changed and tie it to what they said. (Sendr, Smartlead, Martal)
- **Break-up.** Short, plain, no guilt. Often the best reply-getter, and a good place for the wrong-person question. (nearly all)
- **Interest ask.** Ask whether they want to learn more before asking for time. (Gong, Artisan: reported to beat time asks)

## Follow-ups after a conversation (not cold, but common)

- **After a call:** within 24 hours (2 hours after a missed cold call). Three things I heard, invite correction, one dated next step. One email per participant with their own next step. (Martal, Gong, Instantly)
- **After a demo:** one thing I could not tell, what did the rest of the team say? (Martal)
- **No-show:** sorry we missed it, tie to their goal, give a link to rebook. (Gong, Instantly)
- **After a proposal:** reply in the proposal thread; ask a question or offer a clarification; ask the champion for time with the decision maker. (Gong, Instantly)
- **Loop in the decision maker:** one line on team feedback, one reason the meeting serves their priorities, a date. (Gong)
- **After a win:** thank them and ask for one specific introduction. (Gong)

## Phrases to avoid and replace

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Just checking in", "Following up", "Bumping this" | Open with the new fact or question |
| "Did you see my last email?", "Never heard back", "I'm busy too" | A new reason; no guilt |
| "Thoughts?" | One specific, answerable question |
| "Saw you opened my email" | Never mention opens or clicks |
| "Last chance", "time is running out", "ciao, adios" | A real deadline only; otherwise a calm close |
| A fake pretense of a prior conversation ("when we spoke") | Only reference contact that really happened |
| Repeating the first email in different words | One new angle per touch |

## Source conflicts worth flagging to the user

- Bump emails: Jacob Tuwiner and Sendr recommend them; Martal and Coldreach (and reported call-data) say they underperform. Default: avoid them; if used, only very late.
- Morning vs afternoon sending: some say 9 to 12 local time, others say afternoons win for replies. Test both.
- Follow-up length: Gong's own page says longer follow-ups win while Artisan says 25 to 50 words. Default: under about 100 words with real substance.
- Sequence length: from 2 to 9 follow-ups depending on the source.

## Metrics for a sequence

Count replies and positive replies per 100 delivered, per touch. Opens are unreliable. Watch complaints and unsubscribes as you add touches. Stop a sequence that draws angry replies.
