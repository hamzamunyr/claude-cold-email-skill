## 1. Five-part first email | Count: 2 | Source: Clay

Context > Problem and solution > Cost of inaction > Social proof > Light CTA

- One sentence per part, under about 100 words total.
- Best for: outbound to roles where you can see a public signal (hiring, growth, a metric change).
- Weak when: you have no real signal. Generic openers read as spam.

## 2. Four-part scan structure | Count: 6 | Source: Apollo

Personalized opener > Value statement (1 to 2 sentences) > One line of proof > One question to close

- Value statement formula: "we help [who] get [outcome] without [usual pain]".
- Best for: executives who skim. Max about 3 sentences per paragraph, plain text, no attachments.

## 4. Event hook | Count: 19 | Source: Apollo, ZoomInfo, SmartWriter, Warmupinbox, Saleshandy, Truelist, Outplay, Artisan, Salesdorado, TextMagic, Starnus, Oppora, Distribute, Maildoso, FirstSales72

Recent event > the challenge it likely creates > peer result > short call ask

- Best for: funding, launches, acquisitions, big hires. Needs a real, recent event.

## 5. Problem-first | Count: 3 | Source: Apollo, ProProfsDesk, LeadGibbon

Observation > peer who faced the same issue and the result > hint at a different approach > time-boxed call ask

## 6. Peer benchmark | Count: 4 | Source: Apollo, ZoomInfo, Clearout, Starnus

Peer result > note that the method is unusual for the industry > link to the prospect's goal > offer a short breakdown

- Best when you have a strong, shareable case study.

## 7. Industry insight | Count: 5 | Source: Apollo, Outplay, ProProfsDesk, Clearout

Trend > what most companies do > a better way > peer result > curiosity question

- Needs real expertise and a real result, or the contrarian line sounds empty.

## 8. Referral | Count: 35 | Source: Apollo, ZoomInfo, Smartlead, Cognism, HubSpot, Breakcold, Truelist, Cleverly, Reachly, Salesdorado, TextMagic, ProProfsDesk, Clearout, LeadGibbon, MailGenius, Influno, Reachoutly, Distribute, FreebiesMockup, Maildoso, FirstSales72, Follow-up guides, Structure and sequence guides

Mutual contact > outcome achieved > fit with their goal > short call ask

## 10. Quick value drop | Count: 8 | Source: Apollo, Starnus, Distribute, Scalelist, FirstSales72

Their initiative > peer story > the insight written inline > "could this work for you?" question

- Best when you can fit a real insight in two sentences.

## 11. Resource share | Count: 17 | Source: Salesloft, ZoomInfo, SmartWriter, Cognism, Warmupinbox, Truelist, Datalane, Salesbread, Outplay, Reachly, TextMagic, BluLeadz, Waalaxy, Oppora, Semrush

Fresh resource and why you are sending it > how it connects to their pain > open offer of more help

- No hard ask, so it works as a relationship starter. Put the insight in the email or a link, not an attachment. Add one small question to invite a reply.

## 12. Case study first | Count: 34 | Source: Salesloft, ZoomInfo, Coursera, Cognism, Hunter, Salesforge, Salesbread, Outplay, Cleverly, Salesdorado, Clearout, Reachoutly, Distribute, FreebiesMockup, Maildoso, Follow-up guides

Customer result with a link > another win with the same customer > ask for a call with two named days

- Needs a real customer and real numbers. Drop hype phrases like "game changer".

## 14. Collaboration | Count: 31 | Source: Salesloft, Coursera, SmartWriter, Warmupinbox, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Salesbread, DemandCurve, Waalaxy, Clearout, Influno, Reachoutly, Semrush, Outboundly

Specific compliment about something they made > mutual contact > a couple of ideas > short call ask

- Needs a real piece of their content and a real mutual contact.

## 15. AIDA | Count: 19 | Source: Salesloft, Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, SmartWriter, Cognism, HubSpot, Cleverly, Artisan, TextMagic, MailGenius, Reachoutly, Maildoso, FirstSales72, Structure and sequence guides

Attention (subject) > Interest (their post or news) > Desire (shared roadblock plus research) > Action (one CTA)

- Salesloft's version stacks three asks. Keep only one.

## 17. Prior-touch follow-up | Count: 4 | Source: Salesloft, HubSpot, ProProfsDesk, BluLeadz

Reference the voicemail, call or message > two specific time options

- Very short. Add a reason to meet to lift replies.

## 18. Demo recap | Count: 8 | Source: Salesloft, Mailsuite, Follow-up guides

Thanks > one-paragraph value summary > pricing and resources > open offer of more help

- A warm-only pattern. Too long and detailed for a first cold email.

## 19. Pain-point question | Count: 3 | Source: Salesloft, Warmupinbox, HubSpot

Personalized line > question about their current process > peers are investing in this > no-oriented question about hearing how they use it

- The personalized line carries everything. Without it the email is generic.

## 20. Five-block builder | Count: 75 | Source: Woodpecker

Subject > Personalized opener > Problem > Observation or value > Call to action

- The shared skeleton behind every Woodpecker template (69 of 75 use all five blocks). A good base to build from, but it makes emails look alike, so the opener and observation must be genuinely specific.

## 21. Follow-up reminder | Count: 136 | Source: Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, FirstSales, Smartlead, Cognism, LeadsMonky, Warmupinbox, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Salesbread, Cleverly, Reachly, TextMagic, Waalaxy, Clearout, LeadGibbon, Oppora, Influno, Reachoutly, LeadFuze, Semrush, Maildoso, SellingSignals, Outboundly, FirstSales72

One line referencing the earlier email > restate or add one reason > one ask

- Best for: a second or third touch. Keep it under about 80 words and bring something new (a fresh reason, a short resource), not just "checking in".

## 22. PAS (problem, agitate, solution) | Count: 33 | Source: Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, SmartWriter, Cognism, Proposify, Saleshandy, Truelist, Cleverly, Artisan, TextMagic, Starnus, MailGenius, Reachoutly, Distribute, Maildoso, SellingSignals, FirstSales72, Structure and sequence guides

Pain question > deepen the cost of the pain > present the fix with proof > ask to click or chat

- Best for: pains the prospect already feels. Avoid guarantees and insulting framing.

## 23. Before-After-Bridge | Count: 16 | Source: Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, SmartWriter, Artisan, Reachoutly, Maildoso, SellingSignals, FirstSales72, Structure and sequence guides

Describe the current problem > picture the better result > bridge with how you get there > light question

- Best when you can point to a real, checkable observation (for example a metric on their site).

## 25. Social proof pitch | Count: 26 | Source: Woodpecker, HubSpot, Saleshandy, Truelist, Salesbread, Outplay, Mailsuite, TextMagic, ProProfsDesk, Waalaxy, Clearout, Starnus, MailGenius, Distribute, FirstSales72

Industry or pain line > track record with named clients > "we can do the same for you" > ten minute ask

## 26. Event or office-hours invite | Count: 19 | Source: Woodpecker, Artisan, Mailsuite, Waalaxy

Audience-targeted opener > what they will learn, when, who speaks > register or calendar link

- Gives value first. Include a recording or an alternative if they cannot attend.

## 27. Curated list with feedback ask | Count: 4 | Source: Woodpecker

Short list matched to what they said they want > "tell me which are a no, and why" > refine

- Easy to answer and gives you data. Works whenever you can prepare a shortlist.

## 28. Expired listing or stalled situation | Count: 3 | Source: Woodpecker, Reachoutly

Empathize > give one reason it did not work > offer a fresh idea > ask to talk

## 29. Post-win thank you with referral ask | Count: 3 | Source: Woodpecker, Follow-up guides

Congratulate > offer help with leftover questions > ask them to keep you in mind for referrals

## 30. Networking introduction | Count: 13 | Source: Woodpecker, Breakcold, Salesforge, Waalaxy, Reachoutly, Outboundly

Who you are > what you noticed about them > a small coffee, chat or connect ask

- Works when the compliment is specific and the ask is easy to decline.

## 31. Candidate or employer recruiting outreach | Count: 61 | Source: Woodpecker, Smartlead, Warmupinbox, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Clearout, Reachoutly, EmailSneak

To candidates: profile or role trigger > why it fits > a few reasons > time ask. To employers: open role seen > proof and candidates on file > call ask.

## 32. Right-person redirect | Count: 56 | Source: Woodpecker, ZoomInfo, FirstSales, SmartWriter, Cognism, Warmupinbox, HubSpot, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, DemandCurve, Artisan, Reachly, LeadSquared, TextMagic, BluLeadz, Clearout, LeadGibbon, Oppora, LeadFuze, Scalelist, FirstSales72, Structure and sequence guides

Say you are trying to reach whoever handles [area] > ask if that is them or someone else > one line of context with proof > thank them for the pointer

## 33. Standard intro | Count: 5 | Source: ZoomInfo, Cognism, Warmupinbox, FreebiesMockup, Outboundly

Trigger event > the challenge companies in their position usually face > what you do and for whom > "worth a conversation?"

- A three-sentence base. Needs a real trigger, or it reads as generic.

## 34. Competitor mention | Count: 19 | Source: ZoomInfo, Smartlead, SmartWriter, Cognism, Warmupinbox, HubSpot, Proposify, Saleshandy, SmartReach, DemandCurve, Waalaxy, Clearout, LeadGibbon, Reachoutly

Observed tool they use today > why others switched > a similar company's result > interest check

- Do not attack the competitor. Needs a real differentiator and a real switch story.

## 35. No-ask outreach | Count: 6 | Source: ZoomInfo, Starnus, Oppora

Specific compliment or observation > who you work with on the topic > offer to compare notes > state plainly that you ask for nothing

- Good for high-value accounts. Builds goodwill, but expect low replies. Follow up later with a real ask.

## 37. LinkedIn connection follow-up | Count: 5 | Source: ZoomInfo, Smartlead, Cognism, Cleverly, SellingSignals

Thanks for connecting > one detail from their profile or company > what you do plus a proof point > 15 minute ask

## 38. Assume-not-a-priority breakup | Count: 51 | Source: ZoomInfo, FirstSales, Smartlead, LeadsMonky, HubSpot, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Datalane, Salesbread, Cleverly, Reachly, Clearout, Oppora, LeadFuze, SellingSignals, FirstSales72, Follow-up guides, Structure and sequence guides

Say you reached out a few times > assume it is not a priority and stop > invite a reply if that assumption is wrong

- Warns of the last message, which can lift replies. Avoid fake "close your file" gimmicks if they feel manipulative.

## 44. Six-part anatomy | Count: 8 | Source: FirstSales

Subject (short, plain, specific) > opening detail about them > why them (link the detail to a likely problem, one sentence) > what you do (one line, in their words) > one easy question > plain sign-off (just a name)

- Every FirstSales template follows this shape. It doubles as a quick check for any email you write.

## 45. One easy question | Count: 3 | Source: FirstSales

One specific recent detail > "we help [type] get [one result] without [the usual chore]" > a yes-or-no question about whether they deal with the problem

- The reader can answer in a word. Works as a first email when the goal is any reply.

## 49. Engagement signal | Count: 3 | Source: FirstSales, Smartlead, Truelist

You saw them engage with a post on [topic], and say you guessed it is on their mind > how you help in one sentence > offer to share how it works

- Be honest about the signal, and keep it light. Tracking who liked what can feel invasive if the email sounds too knowing.

## 50. Value statement formula | Count: 5 | Source: Apollo, FirstSales

"We help [type of company] get [one result] without [the usual cost or chore]"

- One line, in their words. Used as the "what you do" line in several templates.

## 51. Three-part core (opener, bridge, ask) | Count: 20 | Source: Smartlead, Cleverly

Opener that proves it was written for them > one bridge sentence tying their world to one outcome (with proof if you have it) > one low-friction ask

- Smartlead's view: the first sentence matters most, the bridge is an outcome and not a feature list, and the ask should be specific and bounded.

## 52. Tech-stack trigger | Count: 3 | Source: Smartlead, Salesdorado, Scalelist

A tool they use plus their growth stage > the wall teams at that stage hit > what you do and one proof number > short call ask

- Needs a verifiable tech signal. A wrong guess about their stack kills the email.

## 54. Audit or gap finding | Count: 32 | Source: Smartlead, LeadsMonky, HubSpot, Proposify, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Salesforge, Reachly, BluLeadz, LeadGibbon, MailGenius, Oppora, Reachoutly

A specific finding from a quick check of their ads, content or site > proof you fixed something similar > offer the full findings in a short call

- The finding must be real and specific (real keywords, real pages). A made-up audit is deceptive.

## 55. Event follow-up | Count: 18 | Source: Smartlead, Hunter, SmartReach, Artisan, Clearout, FreebiesMockup, Maildoso

Warm: refer to the actual conversation and the promised resource > a low-pressure line saying you can go deeper if the topic is still live. Cold: a shared session or event > a short link to what you do > short call ask

## 56. Re-engagement | Count: 25 | Source: Smartlead, HubSpot, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Datalane, Cleverly, Reachly, Oppora, FirstSales72, Follow-up guides

Refer to the earlier conversation or a trigger since then > ask whether the topic is still on their radar > offer two clean paths (revisit now or follow up next quarter)

## 60. Three-sentence email | Count: 6 | Source: SmartWriter, HubSpot, Breakcold, Datalane, MailGenius, Reachoutly

Who you are and that you will be quick > what you do for people like them > one small ask

- Not a literal limit. Keep it as close to three sentences as you can.

## 62. Website visitor follow-up | Count: 11 | Source: SmartWriter, Hunter, Salesbread, LeadGibbon, Maildoso, SellingSignals

Acknowledge what they did on your site > recommend related resources > mention one relevant offer > ask for a short call with two time options

- Send a personal note, not a mass list email. Only for real visits.

## 64. Introduction request | Count: 5 | Source: SmartWriter, Warmupinbox, DemandCurve, LeadSquared

Say you saw they know a specific person > ask for an introduction, and say you will send a short note they can forward, or explain the mutual benefit (for example swapping audiences) > keep the ask small

- Only with a real connection. A win-win angle, such as a list swap with non-competing audiences, makes saying yes easier.

## 65. Free product for an honest review | Count: 3 | Source: SmartWriter, SmartReach

Name the specific video or article of theirs you watched or read and what you liked > mention what you just released > offer a free account for an honest review

- Be clear about what you want in return. Do not promise no obligation and then ask for a review.

## 69. Personal video email | Count: 5 | Source: Cognism, HubSpot, Proposify, SmartReach, Reachoutly

One line that shows you read their profile > say you will skip a long email > a short personal video > a virtual coffee ask with two windows

## 70. Humorous breakup with a favor ask | Count: 4 | Source: Cognism, Waalaxy, SellingSignals

Say now may not be the right time and that is fine > share something of yours (a podcast) > ask for a light favor (a subscription) > a playful P.S.

- Only if it suits your brand and the prospect. A joke can fall flat or look flippant.

## 74. Link-building swap | Count: 53 | Source: Warmupinbox, Hunter, SmartReach, Breakcold, BluLeadz

A specific compliment on their article > your related piece and what it adds > ask them to consider linking to it > offer to share theirs with your audience in return

- Only offer an audience you really have. Name its real size.

## 75. Testimonial request | Count: 9 | Source: Warmupinbox, Hunter, Waalaxy, Semrush

Thank a current customer > ask for a testimonial and say how it helps others > offer a small thank-you reward

- For existing customers only. A different job from cold prospecting, so do not reuse a sales template.

## 76. Job application email | Count: 21 | Source: Warmupinbox, Proposify, Hunter, Saleshandy, SmartReach, Salesforge, Reachoutly

Say which role you are applying for > your years of experience and a result that fits their goals > one specific thing you admire about the company > ask for a discussion and name the day you will follow up

- Covers job seekers. Keep it short, specific and truthful.

## 77. Value-led pitch | Count: 4 | Source: HubSpot, Truelist, DemandCurve, Cleverly

An initiative of theirs > teams like them used an approach to reach an outcome with a number > two outcome bullets (no features) > a 15 minute call next week

- Keep features for the demo. Lead with outcomes.

## 78. Warm welcome to a new contact | Count: 4 | Source: HubSpot, Proposify, Salesbread, Waalaxy

Congratulate a new contact at an existing account > mention who at your company worked with their team > offer to share what has worked and quick wins > a short call to compare notes

- Build rapport before selling. Only for real existing relationships.

## 79. Asset-download follow-up | Count: 8 | Source: HubSpot, TextMagic, BluLeadz, Waalaxy, LeadGibbon, SellingSignals

Say what they downloaded > what teams usually do with it, with outcomes > a short walkthrough with a scheduling link, or check they got it and offer to answer questions

- Keep the wording about the content, not about tracking them. "Someone from your company" can feel invasive.

## 81. Multiple-choice breakup | Count: 7 | Source: HubSpot, Proposify, Salesbread, Follow-up guides

Say you have reached out a few times > list three likely reasons for the silence (not a priority, happy now, wrong person) > ask which one it is, and for a referral if it is the wrong person > say you will stop if it is one of the first two

- Easy to answer. A joke version can work but may look flippant.

## 85. Free trial with a no-risk close | Count: 4 | Source: Proposify, Salesbread, DemandCurve

A short intro > one benefit question without the pain they want to avoid > offer a free trial > a three-line close that removes the risk (no card, no commitment, no risk) > a link

- Proposify likes that it does not ask for a call. Only offer a trial you really provide on those terms.

## 87. Careers-page trigger | Count: 4 | Source: Proposify, Salesdorado, Scalelist

Name the open role and one duty from their careers page > say how your product removes that burden > a line of named client proof > three outcome bullets > ask for a call next week

- The first line is custom, the rest can be reused. Use only real client names and results.

## 91. Advice request with an email-only option | Count: 13 | Source: Hunter, DemandCurve, LeadSquared, Waalaxy, Oppora, Reachoutly

Reference something specific they said or wrote > describe your situation and your current thinking in two lines > ask for their view > say they can answer by email if a call is too much

- A follow-up can shrink the ask further to one specific question they can answer in a few sentences. Close with a thank-you note that says what you did with their advice.

## 92. Reconnect through their content | Count: 8 | Source: Hunter, Artisan, LeadSquared

Say a specific article, post or talk of theirs brought you to mind > one specific point you liked and how it links to your work > a light ask to catch up

## 93. Internship pitch with a concrete project idea | Count: 9 | Source: Hunter, Saleshandy, Salesforge, Reachoutly

Show you studied their product or work > describe one specific project or analysis you could do, grounded in their real work > your year, school, skill and one relevant project > ask about the season

- A concrete idea they can picture beats a general "I'd love to learn".

## 94. Congratulation with no ask | Count: 10 | Source: Hunter, SmartReach, LeadSquared, Clearout

Name a specific milestone (an anniversary, a funding round, a launch) > one line on what impressed you > no request at all

- Builds goodwill. Use it before you ask for anything.

## 95. Speculative job inquiry | Count: 7 | Source: Hunter, Reachoutly

Say you are not aware of an open role > one line on relevant experience and a specific accomplishment > the specific thing about their company you admire > ask for a brief conversation

## 96. Win-back with a new reason | Count: 11 | Source: Hunter, Reachly, BluLeadz, Oppora, EmailSneak

Refer to what they used or wanted before > name one concrete change since (a feature, a restructured offer) > a small invitation to take a fresh look

- A genuine change is the reason. Avoid generic "we miss you".

## 97. Investor outreach | Count: 9 | Source: Hunter, DemandCurve, Mailsuite, EmailSneak

Why this person specifically (their expertise or thesis) > one line on the company, traction and round > a short call ask, with a deck on request or attached

- Use a real mutual introduction where possible.

## 98. Question-first intro | Count: 22 | Source: Hunter, SmartReach, Truelist, Artisan, ProProfsDesk, Clearout, Starnus, Reachoutly, Distribute, Scalelist, FirstSales72

Something specific you looked at on their site > a genuine question about how they handle it > say you are not pitching and will say so if you can help

- Gets a conversation going without selling.

## 99. Fit-statement pitch | Count: 6 | Source: Hunter

Say it will be short > explain why they fit your best customer profile > a typical result with a number > two day options for a short call

## 100. Lighter-alternative follow-up | Count: 6 | Source: Hunter

## 102. Permission to share an idea | Count: 9 | Source: Saleshandy, TextMagic, MailGenius

Say you looked at something specific about their work > name a problem teams like theirs hit > say you helped a similar team > ask if it would be useful to share the idea in a short reply

- The ask is permission, not a meeting. Often the lightest possible first ask.

## 104. Podcast guest pitch | Count: 7 | Source: Saleshandy, Salesbread, DemandCurve, Semrush, EmailSneak

Praise a specific episode and the question the host asked > your credibility in one line > two or three concrete topic angles with real examples > offer a short outline or a recorded sample

## 108. Skills-first recruiting outreach | Count: 4 | Source: SmartReach

Cite a specific project or portfolio piece of theirs and what you liked > offer a short skills exercise that mirrors the real work in place of a long application > say there are no whiteboard tests or lengthy rounds > be open about how you found them (including any AI sourcing)

- Honest about automation: say the tool found the profile, but a person reviewed the work.

## 110. Objection answer | Count: 4 | Source: SmartReach, BluLeadz

Acknowledge the objection in one line (budget, already using a competitor, no time) > offer a smaller or easier path (start small, a 10 minute comparison, a done-for-you option) > a similar customer's path > a small ask

## 111. Event meet-up request | Count: 3 | Source: SmartReach

Say you will be at the same event or session > one line on what you can swap notes about > offer a coffee or a 10 minute chat at a named spot

## 112. Quote request for an article | Count: 5 | Source: SmartReach, LeadSquared, Waalaxy

Say what you are writing and where it will appear > ask for one short answer to a specific question > promise credit or a link

- Experts reply more easily when the question is specific and short.

## 113. Affiliate or referral program invite | Count: 6 | Source: SmartReach, Salesforge, Mailsuite, Semrush

Say you saw their content on the topic > list the concrete terms (rate, bonus, resources, a named contact) > a link to join

- Keep terms real and checkable.

## 114. Curiosity-gap question | Count: 5 | Source: Breakcold, MailGenius, Influno, Distribute

Say you noticed they are not using a type of solution and ask why, out of genuine curiosity > say you run a related business and like to learn how others choose > no pitch

- Works because it is not selling. Needs a real, checkable observation.

## 115. Research-interview opener | Count: 4 | Source: Breakcold, DemandCurve, LeadSquared

A personal first line > say you are writing an article or researching a topic > one simple yes-or-no question about their situation

- Only use it if you really are researching. A fake pretext is deceptive.

## 119. Casual one-liner pitch | Count: 4 | Source: Breakcold, Starnus, MailGenius, Reachoutly

"Not sure this is for you, but I figured I would ask" > one line on what you are building and an example > a short question

- Short and honest. Works for early products and founder outreach.

## 123. Permission framing | Count: 3 | Source: Datalane, Oppora

Ask whether it would be out of line to request a short slice of time, name what you would share, and add a plain "no worries if not"

- Turns the ask into a polite question, so a no costs nothing. Works when the relevance is real but a direct call request might feel pushy.

## 125. Partnership win-win pitch | Count: 6 | Source: Salesforge, Influno, Reachoutly, Semrush, Maildoso, EmailSneak

Praise a specific strength of theirs > say who you serve and where the audiences overlap > mention one past partnership and how customers reacted > a short call to explore

- Frame it as mutual benefit. A request that only promotes you is not a partnership.

## 142. Recap and next step after a conversation | Count: 5 | Source: Mailsuite, Follow-up guides

Thank them for a specific talk > summarize two points you agreed on > propose one next step with a time

- Keep it to a few lines and use their words from the call.

## 143. Hype-heavy introduction (avoid) | Count: 3 | Source: Mailsuite, ProProfsDesk

An introduction that leans on words like revolutionary and extraordinary with no proof

- Replace each hype word with one fact or number you can support.

## 145. Six-part signal-first format | Count: 11 | Source: Reachly, Scalelist

Short lowercase subject > greeting > one signal about their company > one consequence > an offer with one real number > one yes-or-no question

- Keep it under 80 words in plain text with no link. The signal must be true for this account.

## 146. Role-matched signal pitch | Count: 5 | Source: Reachly, Scalelist

Match the signal to the role (funding to a founder, hiring posts to a sales head, a new leader to a department head) and state what it usually costs

- The consequence line is what makes the signal matter. Do not reuse a funding opener for someone who did not raise.

## 159. Missed-call follow-up email | Count: 4 | Source: TextMagic, LeadFuze, SellingSignals

Say you tried to call > one short reason > when you will try again and how to reach you

- Only announce a follow-up call you will actually make.

## 175. Guest article pitch | Count: 3 | Source: Influno, Reachoutly, Semrush

Name a post you liked > propose a title that builds on it > a two line summary > offer an outline

- Make it fit their audience and style. Offer the outline, not the full draft.

## Sequences and cadence

**Four-email sequence | Count: 1 | Clay.** Email 1: full pitch. Email 2: how it works. Email 3: change the angle. Email 4: breakup, ask who else should handle it.

**Five-touch cadence | Count: 1 | Apollo.** Day 0 cold email. About day 3: share a useful resource. About a week later: a different angle. About a week later: a soft breakup. About 3 months later: re-engage with a new trigger.

**Cadence by list size | Count: 1 | Clay.**
- Large list (tens of thousands): one or two emails inside a week, then rest 45 days or more.
- Small list (a few thousand): about one useful email a month, and mix in other channels. Each email should help even if they are not ready to buy.

**Multichannel follow-up | Count: 1 | Salesloft.** If a call goes unanswered, send an email that references the voicemail. Also follow up after connecting on LinkedIn or social media.

**30/30/50 rule | Count: 1 | Coursera.** A rule of thumb that about 30 percent of success comes from researching the right audience, about 30 percent from the email's content and structure, and about 50 percent from following up. Coursera cites a Backlinko study saying follow-ups are about twice as likely to get a response as the first email (not verified here). It suggests following up about a week after the first email, and sending on weekday mornings.

**Follow-up volume and timing | Count: 1 | ZoomInfo.** Plan three to five follow-ups a few days apart before sending the first email. Each follow-up should add a new angle or value, and most replies arrive after the second or third touch. Weekday mornings (8 to 10 AM in the prospect's time zone) are suggested, which matches Apollo's advice. ZoomInfo also suggests keeping emails under about 150 words and testing subject lines and body copy as separate variables.

**Four-step sequence (days 0, 3, 7, 12) | Count: 1 | FirstSales.** Day 0: one easy question. Day 3: a follow-up with a new angle (a cost, a risk or time saved) and one line of proof you can back up. Day 7: a short nudge with two easy answers. Day 12: a polite last email that says you will stop. Plan the follow-ups when you write the first email, and stop the sequence as soon as the person replies or opts out. Change one thing at a time when testing (the subject or the ask, not both).

**Three-email follow-up (days 3, 7, 14) | Count: 1 | LeadsMonky.** Day 3: a short reminder that promises something useful. Day 7: a three-point observation about their business, useful whether or not you work together. Day 14: a break-up that assumes the timing is wrong and leaves the door open. LeadsMonky claims three follow-ups raise response by about 28% and that the break-up gets the highest reply rate (unverified). Its day 3 email is a plain bump, which other sources warn against, so add something new instead.

**Deliverability basics | Count: 2 | ZoomInfo, LeadsMonky.** Send from a separate domain, not your main business domain. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Warm up new domains gradually (about 10 to 20 emails a day, growing over several weeks). Keep bounce rate low (about 2 to 3% or under) by verifying lists. Use a human sender name, plain text and an opt-out. These claims come from vendors selling email tools.

**HubSpot follow-up and CTA guidance | Count: 1 | HubSpot.** Aim for about three to five follow-ups over roughly 10 to 21 days, combined with calls and social touches. Beyond that, replies drop unless each touch adds something new. For senior stakeholders, soften the ask (a short reply, a forwarded intro) before asking for a full demo. HubSpot claims 3 to 5% is an average cold B2B reply rate and that the best exceed 10%, and that referrals get about four times more replies (all unverified).

**Truelist cadence and quality guidance | Count: 1 | Truelist.** Use three to five touches spaced three to five business days apart, then stop and revisit next quarter with a new angle. Long sequences with several "bumping this up" emails are the fastest route to spam flags. Reply benchmarks it gives (unverified): 3 to 8% normal, 8 to 15% good, over 15% exceptional, and under 2% usually means a deliverability or list problem, not the copy. Aim for 50 to 110 words, authenticate your domain, send from a separate domain and warm it slowly.

**Smartlead follow-up and testing guidance | Count: 1 | Smartlead.** Use four to six touches for B2B, and give each follow-up a new angle (a new insight, a different framing, a related resource or a different stakeholder). Day 3 can add a new insight, day 7 a different question, then a warm break-up. Practitioners cited by the page report this order of impact (unverified): the offer you make beats the template, the first line beats the subject line, specificity beats length, and a new follow-up angle beats follow-up timing. The page also says reply rate is the main metric, and that open rate is increasingly unreliable.

**Pain ordering across a sequence | Count: 1 | Salesbread.** List every problem your product solves, rank by how often it occurs, lead the first email with the most common one, then use the next in the follow-up.

**Micro-campaign split test | Count: 1 | Salesbread.** Send one email and one follow-up to two separate lists with two versions, then compare both the copy and the list.

**Follow-up spacing | Count: 2 | Salesbread, Copy.ai.** Space touches on a widening gap (Salesbread suggests the Fibonacci sequence), and plan three or four follow-ups that each add something. Copy.ai advises mentioning in the email that you will follow up.

**Four-touch cadence | Count: 1 | Cleverly.** Day 1 first email, day 4 value add in the same thread, day 9 honest re-engage that offers to stop, day 14 breakup. Run one template for 50 to 100 sends before changing it, and change one thing at a time.

**Two-email sequence with a channel pivot | Count: 1 | Reachly.** Email 1 on day 1 in the six-part format, a one-line second email 5 to 7 days later asking whether it is of interest or belongs to a colleague, then a LinkedIn note to people active there, then stop. Re-engage the same list 1.5 to 2.5 months later with a new angle or new proof.

**Grid check before sending | Count: 1 | Salesdorado.** Score a draft on six points: asks for interest not a time slot, under 100 words, no product description, short subject without numbers, no ROI promise, a specific verifiable trigger. The page says to bin a template that fails two or more. Its figures are cited from other sources and unverified here.

**Email plus LinkedIn ten-day plan | Count: 1 | Starnus.** Day 1 email, day 2 LinkedIn connection note referencing the email, day 5 a thoughtful comment on their post, day 7 email follow-up with a proof point, day 10 a short breakup. The page suggests 3 to 5 touches over 10 to 14 days and 50 to 125 words per email.

**Outreach timing and sending setup | Count: 1 | Influno.** Plan two or three follow-ups over about two weeks: a reminder after 4 to 7 days, then one that adds a new angle or resource. Send Tuesday to Thursday mornings in the recipient's time zone, and use a separate, properly warmed domain for cold outreach so the main domain stays safe.

**Fourteen-day trigger sequence with a payload change | Count: 1 | Distribute.** Day 1 email tied to a live trigger, day 4 follow-up that adds something useful, day 7 LinkedIn touch, day 10 email with proof, day 14 closeout with an easy out. Change the payload each time. Track positive replies and cost per positive reply. Strong copy with weak positive replies usually means targeting, and strong targeting with weak opens usually means deliverability or subject lines.

**Spaced follow-ups that shrink | Count: 1 | Semrush.** Wait three to five business days between messages and stop at three follow-ups. Change the message each time, and let each one get shorter, since the earlier email sits below it in the thread.

**Measure per 100 recipients | Count: 1 | Scalelist.** Count replies per 100 recipients, per template, on the same kind of list, and change one thing at a time. Published averages use different denominators (per email sent, per recipient, per sequence), so compare your own templates against each other, not against one number.

**Follow-up playbook | Count: 15 | Follow-up guides.** The full follow-up guidance (count, timing, jobs per touch, phrases to avoid, source conflicts) is in followups.md. Short version: 3 follow-ups, first after 2 to 3 business days, gaps widening (about 2, 4, 7, 14), each one adds something new, reply in the thread, one question, close with a short no-guilt breakup.

**2-4-7-14 cadence | Count: 2 | Mixmax, GMass.** First follow-up 2 days after, then 4, 7 and 14 days after the previous. GMass adds a fifth at 30 days.

**Seniority spacing | Count: 1 | Martal.** SMB owner every 3 to 5 days (sequence inside two weeks), mid-market director every 4 to 7 days (18 to 25 days), enterprise VP every 7 to 10 days (25 to 35), C-suite every 10 to 14 days (35 to 45).

**Six-touch multichannel sequence | Count: 1 | Martal.** Day 0 email, day 2 to 3 email with new information, day 5 to 7 phone or LinkedIn, day 9 to 12 email with a new framing or proof, day 14 to 18 phone, day 21 to 25 break-up email.

**Widening-gap sequence | Count: 1 | lemlist.** 2 days between emails 1 and 2, 4 days, 4 days, then 5 or more days, for 10 to 25 days total. Swap some emails for other channels instead of adding touches.

**Three-step cold follow-up | Count: 1 | Instantly.** Day 3 a value bump, day 7 social proof, day 12 a breakup. Proposal follow-ups run 4 to 5 touches over 3 to 4 weeks.

**Weekly follow-up for freelancers | Count: 1 | Ashley Gainer.** One week, another week, another week, then stop after the third. Honor any deadline in a job post, and set a reminder when told to come back later.

**Monthly drip after the sequence | Count: 1 | Jacob Tuwiner.** After four follow-ups, one email a month until a reply or a request to stop (use only if each one is useful and compliant).

**Signal-driven sequence | Count: 1 | Autobound.** 4 to 7 emails: email 1 to 2 after 2 to 3 business days, 2 to 3 after 3 to 4, 3 to 4 after 5 to 7, then 7 to 14 days. Stop when they reply, the signal goes stale, a stronger signal appears or they leave the company.

**Valid A/B test standard | Count: 1 | SalesHive.** A hypothesis, one changed variable, about 250 contacts per variant as a floor (500 or more preferred), 95% confidence, a two to six week run, and the metric that matches the test (opens for subjects, replies for body or offer, meetings booked for the call to action).

**Situation-based sequences | Count: 5 | Instantly.** Different 3-email sequences for new leads, referrals, meeting requests, non-responders and interested-but-quiet prospects, over about two weeks (day 1, days 3 to 4, days 6 to 8, days 10 to 14), each follow-up with a fresh reason, stopping as soon as they say yes or no.

**Same-thread follow-ups with stop on reply | Count: 1 | Woodpecker.** Two to three follow-ups in the same thread, each with a new reason; stop automation once the prospect replies.
