Rule: the first line is about the prospect, not you. Skip empty openers ("hope you're well", "I know you're busy", "I found you on LinkedIn"). Vague observations feel weaker than specific ones. Avoid rumor-style openers (hearsay about the prospect) unless the fact is verifiable and the tone is kind.

Hunter library statistics (from its 510 templates): in the curated set of 228 (six per subcategory), about 90% of subjects carry a custom detail field, subjects average about 6 words, only about 7% are questions, and none use "hope you are well" openers. In the older community-submitted set of 282, about 34% of subjects carry a custom field, about 18% are questions, 13% open with "hope you are well" and 14% open with "my name is". Specific, short and detail-led subjects dominate the better group.

Extra subject guidance from SmartWriter (not verified): a Leadium analysis it cites says subject lines of no more than four words performed best. It lists phrases that often trigger spam filters ("Click Here", "Best Price", "Winner", "Money Making", "Double Your", "Why Pay More?"), and says a friendly conversational subject beats a stiff one.

Pairing rule (Smartlead): the subject and the first line should read as one continuous thought. If the subject says one thing and the email opens on another, you lose the reader. Match the subject to the opener: reference what you found, name the event, name the competitor, lead with proof, name the connection, or ask the question.

Pre-header (the preview line after the subject): Coursera suggests using it for a real industry insight or statistic, not a repeat of the subject.

Guidelines (claims from Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker, ZoomInfo and Coursera, not verified): about 7 words or under 50 characters, specific beats vague, a question can lift curiosity when it fits their real situation, company name helps, no punctuation tricks, no emojis, no hype or spam words ("free", "limited time", "revolutionary", "game-changing"). Woodpecker's own examples often break this, so do not copy them blindly.
