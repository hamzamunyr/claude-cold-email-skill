# Hooks and Subject Lines

Emails analyzed so far: 21 (Clay, Apollo, Salesloft).

## Opening line patterns

| Pattern | Formula | Count | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger event plus insight | "I noticed [event]. In my experience that usually means [insight]." | 4 | Clay, Apollo |
| Recent event hook | Congratulate on funding, a launch, a hire or an award, then name the challenge it likely creates | 1 | Apollo |
| Job posting hook | Reference a duty in their open role that points at a problem you solve | 2 | Clay |
| Headcount growth hook | Mention a visible team expansion and ask how they will handle the next step | 2 | Clay |
| Website visit hook | "I looked through your site and put together a few ideas" | 2 | Clay |
| Visitor hook | Acknowledge that they visited or downloaded from you, then offer a next step | 1 | Salesloft |
| Company page hook | Refer to something seen on their LinkedIn or company page, then ask a question | 1 | Clay |
| LinkedIn post hook | Refer to a specific post of theirs, then tie it to a shared roadblock | 1 | Salesloft |
| Podcast or content compliment | Praise a specific episode or piece, then bridge to an idea | 1 | Salesloft |
| Peer result hook | Lead with a similar company's or customer's result and tie it to their goal | 3 | Apollo, Salesloft |
| Industry trend hook | Name a trend affecting their market, then say the usual response falls short | 1 | Apollo |
| Mutual connection hook | Open with a real shared contact and an outcome | 2 | Apollo, Salesloft |
| Shared-problem opener | "Many people in your role struggle with [specific problem]" | 1 | Salesloft |
| Fresh resource hook | "We just got [new data]. Thought it could help with [their pain]" | 1 | Salesloft |
| Prior touch hook | Reference a voicemail, call or earlier message | 1 | Salesloft |
| Value drop hook | Give the insight in the email itself and say how long it takes to read | 1 | Apollo |
| Metric change hook | Cite a public data point that moved (for example web traffic) | 1 | Clay |
| New role hook | Congratulate on a new role, then tie it to a hiring signal | 1 | Clay |

Rule: the first line is about the prospect, not you. Skip empty openers ("hope you're well", "I know you're busy", "I found you on LinkedIn"). Vague observations feel weaker than specific ones.

## Subject line patterns

| Pattern | Example shape | Count | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event congrats | "Congrats on [specific event]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Question about a topic | "Quick question about [specific area]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Peer result | "How [peer company] hit [metric]" | 2 | Apollo |
| Trend thought | "[First name], quick thought on [trend]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Mutual connection | "[Name] suggested I reach out" | 1 | Apollo |
| Short read | "2-min read: [insight] for [company]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Initiative reference | "About your [specific initiative]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Noticed at company | "Noticed this at [company]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Thought of you | "[First name], thought of [company] when I saw this" | 1 | Apollo |
| One-word subject | A single plain word tied to their topic | 1 (claimed 87% higher reply rate, unverified) | Salesloft |

Guidelines (claims from Apollo and Salesloft, not verified): around 7 words or under 50 characters, specific beats vague, company name helps, no urgency, no punctuation tricks, no emojis, no hype words such as "revolutionary" or "game-changing".
