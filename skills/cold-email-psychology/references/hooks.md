# Hooks and Subject Lines

Emails analyzed so far: 96 (Clay, Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker). Woodpecker counts are approximate.

## Opening line patterns

| Pattern | Formula | Count | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior touch hook | "Following up on my last email about [topic]" plus one new reason | 27 | Salesloft, Woodpecker |
| Job posting or open role hook | Reference an open role or hiring post that points at a problem you solve | 7 | Clay, Woodpecker |
| Trigger event plus insight | "I noticed [event]. In my experience that usually means [insight]." | 5 | Clay, Apollo, Woodpecker |
| Empathy opener | Name the frustration first ("I know a stalled listing is frustrating") | 6 | Woodpecker |
| Pain question opener | Start with a question about the problem they live with | 5 | Woodpecker |
| Milestone congrats hook | Congratulate on a sale, purchase, graduation or achievement, then tie to the next step | 4 | Woodpecker |
| Headcount growth hook | Mention a visible team expansion and ask how they will handle the next step | 2 | Clay |
| Website visit hook | "I looked through your site and put together a few ideas" | 2 | Clay |
| Peer result hook | Lead with a similar company's or customer's result and tie it to their goal | 3 | Apollo, Salesloft |
| Mutual connection hook | Open with a real shared contact and an outcome | 5 | Apollo, Salesloft, Woodpecker |
| LinkedIn post or profile hook | Refer to a specific post or profile detail, then tie it to a shared roadblock or fit | 6 | Salesloft, Woodpecker |
| Shared event or meeting hook | Refer to an event you both attend or a place you met | 3 | Woodpecker |
| Self-aware opener | Acknowledge the interruption in one light line ("you probably get a lot of these") | 3 | Woodpecker |
| Similarity opener | Mention a real shared role or interest | 2 | Woodpecker |
| Metric check hook | Cite a public data point that moved or that you checked (traffic, authority score) | 3 | Clay, Woodpecker |
| Recent event hook | Congratulate on funding, a launch, a hire or an award, then name the challenge it likely creates | 1 | Apollo |
| Industry trend hook | Name a trend or market change affecting their market, then say the usual response falls short | 2 | Apollo, Woodpecker |
| Visitor hook | Acknowledge that they visited or downloaded from you, then offer a next step | 1 | Salesloft |
| Thank-you response hook | Thank them for a demo request or trial signup and give the next step | 2 | Woodpecker |
| Podcast or content compliment | Praise a specific episode or piece, then bridge to an idea | 1 | Salesloft |
| Company page hook | Refer to something seen on their LinkedIn or company page, then ask a question | 1 | Clay |
| Shared-problem opener | "Many people in your role struggle with [specific problem]" | 1 | Salesloft |
| Fresh resource hook | "We just got [new data]. Thought it could help with [their pain]" | 1 | Salesloft |
| Value drop hook | Give the insight in the email itself and say how long it takes to read | 1 | Apollo |
| New role hook | Congratulate on a new role, then tie it to a hiring signal | 1 | Clay |

Rule: the first line is about the prospect, not you. Skip empty openers ("hope you're well", "I know you're busy", "I found you on LinkedIn"). Vague observations feel weaker than specific ones. Avoid rumor-style openers (hearsay about the prospect) unless the fact is verifiable and the tone is kind.

## Subject line patterns

| Pattern | Example shape | Count | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question about their situation | "Still looking for a new [role]?" or "Ready to try selling again?" | 26 | Woodpecker |
| Plain benefit or offer | "We'll deliver the candidates you need" | 15 | Woodpecker |
| Role-targeted | "Looking for work as a [role]?" | 10 | Woodpecker |
| Still-question follow-up | "Still interested in talking?" | 7 | Woodpecker |
| Urgency or deadline | "Don't forget about our Open House on [date]!" | 6 | Woodpecker |
| Event or date | "Let's connect at [event]" | 5 | Woodpecker |
| Mutual connection | "[Name] suggested I reach out" | 4 | Apollo, Woodpecker |
| Thank-you | "Thanks for requesting a demo" | 2 | Woodpecker |
| Peer result | "How [peer company] hit [metric]" | 2 | Apollo |
| Event congrats | "Congrats on [specific event]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Question about a topic | "Quick question about [specific area]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Trend thought | "[First name], quick thought on [trend]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Short read | "2-min read: [insight] for [company]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Initiative reference | "About your [specific initiative]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Noticed at company | "Noticed this at [company]" | 1 | Apollo |
| Thought of you | "[First name], thought of [company] when I saw this" | 1 | Apollo |
| One-word subject | A single plain word tied to their topic | 1 (claimed 87% higher reply rate, unverified) | Salesloft |

Guidelines (claims from Apollo, Salesloft and Woodpecker tips, not verified): around 7 words or under 50 characters, specific beats vague, a question can lift curiosity when it fits their real situation, company name helps, no punctuation tricks, no emojis, no hype or spam words ("free", "limited time", "revolutionary", "game-changing"). Woodpecker's own examples often break this, so do not copy them blindly.
