## How to apply each one

**Specificity.** Open with something observed about the prospect: a job post, a hiring spree, their website, a metric change. State what it likely means for them. Add a number only if it is real.

**Reciprocity.** Give something useful and free first: a short video, a webinar invite, or ideas written for their business. Then ask.

**Curiosity gap.** Tease a result or a list without giving everything. Ask about something they have probably never measured, or promise to show "how".

**Commitment minimization.** Make the first yes tiny: "could I send a short video?" beats "can we book 30 minutes?"

**Effort reduction.** Keep it short enough to read in about 20 seconds. Cut any sentence that does not earn its place.

**Unique mechanism.** Name the one thing you do that a competitor cannot say. Concrete beats generic.

**Social proof.** Best is a company the prospect knows plus a result and timeframe. Next best is a similar company. Never invent names or numbers.

**Loss aversion.** Ask a question that shows what they lose by waiting, such as time, money, or missed pipeline. Phrase it as a question, not an accusation.

**Authority by association.** Mention real, verifiable groups or people you have worked with or taught. Skip it if you cannot back it up.

**Topical hook.** Tie the email to a subject already on their mind, then connect it to a concrete offer. Avoid broad hype with no link to their business.

**Easy exit.** In a final follow-up, ask who else should handle this. It takes pressure off and often routes you to the right person.
