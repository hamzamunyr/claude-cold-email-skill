## How to apply each one

**Specificity.** Open with something observed about the prospect: a job post, a hiring spree, a metric change. State what it likely means for them. Add one number only if it is real.

**Social proof.** Best is a company the prospect knows plus a result and timeframe. Next best is a company of similar size or industry. Never invent names or numbers.

**Loss aversion.** Ask a question that shows what they lose by waiting, such as time, money, or missed pipeline. Make it a question, not an accusation.

**Curiosity gap.** Point at something the prospect has probably never measured, or promise to show "how" without giving the whole answer.

**Reciprocity.** Offer something useful and free (a short video, an insight, a teardown) before asking for time.

**Commitment minimization.** Make the first yes tiny: "would that be useful?" beats "can we book 30 minutes?"

**Effort reduction.** Keep it short enough to read in about 20 seconds. Cut any sentence that does not earn its place.

**Unique mechanism.** Name the one thing you do that a competitor cannot say. Concrete beats generic.

**Easy exit.** In a final follow-up, ask who else should handle this. It takes pressure off and often routes you to the right person.
