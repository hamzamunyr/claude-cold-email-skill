## How to apply each one

**Specificity.** Open with something observed about the prospect: a job post, a hiring spree, a funding event, their website, a post, a podcast, a metric change. State what it likely means for them. Add a number only if it is real.

**Effort reduction.** Keep it short enough to read in about 20 seconds. Cut any sentence that does not earn its place.

**Commitment minimization.** Make the first yes tiny. Name a short duration ("2 minutes", "10 minutes") or offer something small to receive. A small ask beats "can we book 30 minutes?"

**Curiosity gap.** Tease a result, a method or a list without giving everything. Ask about something they have probably never measured, or promise to show "how".

**Reciprocity.** Give something useful and free first: a short video, a breakdown, an insight, a link, or ideas written for their business. Then ask.

**Social proof.** Best is a company the prospect knows plus a result and timeframe. Next best is a similar company, or the fact that peers are adopting something. Never invent names or numbers.

**Unique mechanism.** Name the one thing you do that a competitor cannot say. Concrete beats generic.

**Loss aversion.** Ask a question that shows what they lose by waiting, such as time, money, or missed pipeline. Phrase it as a question, not an accusation.

**Authority.** Mention real, verifiable groups you have worked with, original data or research, or clear expertise on a trend. Skip it if you cannot back it up.

**Contrarian angle.** Say the usual approach falls short, then hint at a different one. Only use it if you can back the claim up.

**Competitive comparison.** Show how a similar company is doing and how that compares to the prospect's goal. Executives watch peers closely.

**Borrowed trust.** Use a real mutual contact who agreed to be named. Never imply a referral that did not happen.

**Forced choice.** Offer two specific options (two days, two formats) instead of an open "when are you free?". Choosing is easier than starting from nothing.

**Normalization.** Say that many people in their role face the same problem. It makes admitting the problem feel safe. Name one real problem, not a vague one.

**Easy exit.** In a final follow-up, ask who else should handle this, or give a polite way out. It takes pressure off and often routes you to the right person.

**Liking through a specific compliment.** Praise something real and specific they made or said, such as a podcast episode. Generic praise reads as flattery.

**Personal video.** A short intro video makes the email feel human. Best for warm leads who already showed interest.

**Intent signal.** When a prospect visited your site or downloaded something, say what you noticed and offer the next step. Only use this for real behavior.

**No-oriented question.** A question where "no" is the easy, safe answer (for example, are you against hearing how peers do this) can lower resistance. Keep the wording simple to avoid double negatives.

**Consistency across channels.** Refer to an earlier call, voicemail or message so the email feels like part of one conversation.

**Price transparency.** Be open about pricing when the buyer is already evaluating. It builds trust but is too much for a first cold email.

**Topical hook.** Tie the email to a subject already on their mind, then connect it to a concrete offer. Avoid broad hype with no link to their business.
