# Reply Handling

Use this when the user pastes a reply to a cold email and asks what to say. The same honesty rules apply as in the rest of the skill: no invented facts, results, customers, pricing, discounts, deadlines or features; no guilt or pressure; no pretending; nothing sent without the user's approval.

## 1. Procedure

1. **Classify the reply** (table below). If it is ambiguous, say so and pick the safest reading.
2. **Check for opt-outs first.** "Stop", "unsubscribe", "remove me", "do not contact me" and clear hostile refusals are opt-outs. Do not write a persuasive reply. Recommend adding them to the suppression list the same day and, at most, a one-line confirmation if appropriate. Do not argue.
3. **Use only facts the user supplied** (pricing, features, results, availability). If the reply asks for something the user has not provided, ask the user for it rather than guessing.
4. **Match their length and tone.** A one-line reply gets a short answer.
5. **Make the next step small and optional.**
6. **Log the outcome** for the feedback loop (positive, neutral, negative, referral, opt-out, objection type) if the user keeps results (performance-analytics.md).

## 2. Reply types and responses

| Reply | What it usually means | What to do | What not to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Not interested" | A no, sometimes a no for now | Thank them briefly, confirm you will stop, optionally ask a single useful question only if it is a genuine learning question ("Was it timing or fit?"). Mark as negative; suppress unless they say otherwise | Argue, re-pitch, guilt, or send another email in the sequence |
| "Send more info" | Interest or a polite brush-off | Send something short and relevant that the user really has (a one-page summary, a short example), then ask one question that qualifies ("Is this for [specific problem] or something else?") | A long deck dump, unsupported claims, or a hard push for a meeting in the same message |
| "What's the price?" | Genuine interest or screening | Give the real price or a real range the user supplied, and what it includes. If pricing depends on factors, say which, and ask the one question needed to quote. If the user has not supplied pricing, ask the user | Inventing a price, a discount, a deadline or a "special offer" |
| "We already use X" | A real incumbent, or a brush-off | Acknowledge respectfully. Ask a genuine question about what is working or missing, or ask whether it is worth revisiting at renewal. Do not disparage X. Offer a specific, true difference only if the user has one | Claiming X is bad, claiming switching is easy or cheap without proof, or invented comparisons |
| "Not the right person" | A route, often valuable | Thank them and ask who owns it, or whether they can forward a two-line note. Make forwarding easy (draft a short forwardable note). Keep it brief and kind | Pushing them to do work, or guessing the right person's name |
| "Reach out next quarter" or "Not now" | A soft no or genuine timing | Confirm the date, say you will contact them then, and offer one useful line now if it helps. Add a dated reminder for the user. Do not email before the date | Following up early, or asking to meet now anyway |
| Positive reply (interested, "yes", "tell me more", "let's talk") | Interest | Reply quickly. Confirm what they asked, answer plainly, and propose a concrete next step (two time windows or a calendar link now that interest is shown). Ask only what is needed to prepare | Burying them in questions, adding new claims, or sending a calendar link without context |
| Out-of-office or auto-reply | Not a response | Do not count it as interest. Pause the sequence until they return if the date is given | Replying to the auto-responder, or treating it as an opt-out or a positive |
| "How did you get my email?" | A compliance and trust test | Answer truthfully (the real source), apologize if appropriate, offer to remove them, and offer an opt-out. Escalate to the user if the data source is unclear | Evading or inventing a source |
| Hostile or abusive reply | Complaint risk | Do not reply in kind. Suppress immediately and note it. At most a short, polite confirmation | Defending, joking, or re-engaging |
| Question about the offer (specific) | Real interest | Answer only what the user has evidenced; say "I will confirm" and ask the user for the detail you lack | Guessing details to seem helpful |

## 3. Example responses (placeholders to be filled with real, supplied facts)

**Not interested**

> Thanks for letting me know, [First name]. I will not email again. If it is useful for me to know, was it more about timing or fit? Either way, all the best.

**Send more info**

> Happy to. Here is a one-page summary: [real link or attachment you have]. It covers [what it really covers]. Is the main thing you are trying to solve [specific problem], or something else?

**What's the price?**

> [Plan or range the user supplied] per [unit], which includes [what is included]. Pricing depends on [factor]. If you tell me [one detail], I can give you the exact figure.

**We already use X**

> That makes sense, and X is a common choice. Out of curiosity, what is working well with it, and is there anything you wish it did? If it is useful, I can check back around your next renewal.

**Not the right person**

> Thanks, [First name]. Who would be the right person to talk to about [problem]? If it is easier, here are two lines you can forward: [short forwardable note, true and neutral].

**Next quarter**

> Understood. I will reach out in [month]. I will not email before then.

**Positive reply**

> Great, thanks for replying. To keep it quick: [one-line answer to what they asked]. Would [window 1] or [window 2] work for a [short duration] call? If easier, here is a link: [calendar link].

## 4. Safeguards

- Do not use a reply to bait a commitment ("since you replied, you agreed to a call").
- If the user wants to say something the evidence does not support, say so and offer an honest version.
- Treat any request to stop as final, even if the reply is rude or ambiguous.
- Draft replies for the user's review. Do not send them.
- If the reply raises a legal or complaint concern (a threat, a regulator, "report you"), recommend the user stop and consult the sender's compliance owner or a lawyer.

## 5. Output format

1. **Classification** and the reason in one line.
2. **Recommended action** (reply, suppress, pause, escalate).
3. **Draft reply**, with [brackets] for facts the user must supply.
4. **Flags**: any claim you could not support and what is needed.
